Philox4x32-10 of one 128-bit counter under the 64-bit key: the four words of one draw.

Philox4x32 (Salmon, Moraes, Dror and Shaw, “Parallel random numbers: as easy as 1, 2, 3”, SC11) is ten rounds, as Random123’s philox4x32_10, of (c₀, c₁, c₂, c₃) → (hi(M₁c₂) ⊕ c₁ ⊕ k₀, lo(M₁c₂), hi(M₀c₀) ⊕ c₃ ⊕ k₁, lo(M₀c₀)), with M₀ = 0xD2511F53 and M₁ = 0xCD9E8D57, the key bumped by the Weyl constants (0x9E3779B9, 0xBB67AE85) before every round after the first. It keeps no state: the words are a pure function of the counter and the key, matching Random123’s known answers.

This is the draw CellNoise::add_to_row makes for each correlation pair, sixteen channels at a time; one counter at a time it is the reference other implementations of the same noise (another batch width, a GPU kernel) are compared with, bit for bit.

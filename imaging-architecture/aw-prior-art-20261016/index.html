    AW-projection prior art: what made the pre-refactor VLASS rows fast, and what happened to it¶

    Truth class: non-normative primary-source research Date: 2026-10-16 Work issue: #449 (the 10x programme)

    Research notes, 2026-10-16. A read-only study of the casa-rs repository at 6a9e83f36b and its GitHub issues. Nothing was built or run.

    The figures in section 4 are the study's own estimates, not measurements. The open owner decisions this raises are recorded on #449: whether the recovery contract's landmarks are retired, and how ADR-0016 decision 2 treats a tap-plan cache.

    Abbreviations: - HP is the historical VLASS plan docs/tutorial-parity/vlass-imaging-optimization-plan.md as it stood at 873776457e^. It is 8,341 lines and was deleted by 873776457e on 2026-08-13. Line numbers below refer to that version. - RC is docs/tutorial-parity/vlass-imaging-recovery-contract.md at HEAD. - FP is docs/imaging-architecture/imaging-foundation-plan-20261007.md at HEAD. - L is crates/casa-imaging/src/lib.rs at d2d0c0dad5, the last August production commit before the main merge. The crate is 86,874 lines; the same symbols are still present in 96,376 lines at the main merge ba0354ef93.

    0. The most important finding: two deletions, not one¶

    The fast VLASS mechanisms were not removed by the imaging-foundation refactor.

    Lineage. These are verified with git merge-base --is-ancestor:

    41caf8cd0a (07-29 work, pushed 08-02) → 8667b5760d (casa-c) → 388161fd29 → f2abbb98bd → 4d787cb1fb (casa-d checkpoint) → d2d0c0dad5 → ba0354ef93 (main merge, 2026-08-13).

    First deletion: the August ADR-0009 cutover. - Two commits deleted the whole legacy casa-imaging crate (96,750 lines) and the casars-imager monolith (82,961 lines): - fff9c2d553, "complete native continuum cutover", 2026-08-25; - 27b9b602fe, "complete final-owner cutover", 2026-08-25. - The programme behind them was the #486 imaging-architecture programme, with its migration matrix and "transfer ratchet". - At that cutover the migration matrix marked AW as "typed unavailable boundary; no production implementation". AW was rebuilt later in T50–T53 (#536–#539). The matrix is at 27b9b602fe:resources/imaging-architecture/migration-matrix.json. - What went with the crate: - the frozen-base image-response cache; - the compact replay cache; - Metal resident/global/grouped replay; - the sparse MT-MFS right-hand side (RHS); - radix MADFM; - the NEON 2×2 sparse initial grid; - compensated Metal AW gridding. - git log -S'struct AwProjectImageResponseCache' shows the struct was added by 41caf8cd0a and removed by 27b9b602fe.

    What #486 re-implemented. - A generic gridded-normal replay: - crates/casa-imaging-reconstruction/src/gridded_normal_operator.rs, added by 712b399f8e (2026-08-24) and e413729fa8 (2026-08-28); - 40-byte scalar records and 96-byte AW records (AW_GRIDDED_NORMAL_OPERATOR_RECORD_BYTES, e714fd2339 version, around line 85); - crates/casa-imaging-runtime/src/complete_data_operator.rs. - complete_data_operator/metal_normal.rs (565b79a54d, 2026-10-04, standard MFS).

    Second deletion: IF-2 under D1. - e7d29c8e5b (IF-2, 2026-10-07, "run every request through the major-cycle pass and delete the old drivers") deleted the gridded-normal replay and metal_normal.rs. - reconstruction/src/image_response.rs at e714fd2339 is not the response cache. It is the flat-noise/flat-sky PB normalisation (MinorCycleImageResponse). It moved to products in e83759240d.

    When the fast path was last production. - The August production freeze (PR #464, merged 2026-08-13) had already chosen "role-segmented source-major Metal initial gridding with CPU residual refresh" for full geometry. - The VLASS recovery retrospective (4a29cc1d52:docs/tutorial-parity/vlass-imaging-recovery-retrospective.md) says the CPU residual backend "produced a scientifically accepted result after compressed and Metal-residual candidates failed". - The accepted 12,150² all-63-field, 16-SPW clean took about 40,412 s against CASA-B's 82,351.8 s, about 2.0×. The no-new-swap guard was waived (PR #464; TESTING.md at 6ce6fad837).

    No pre-IF build ever ran the #625 row (63 fields, 16 SPWs, 4096², niter 2000) for time. - #625 was queued and never activated. - An August cleaned 4096²/16-SPW/63-field run (r22) passed science (PR #464), but no wall time is recorded in the repository, PR #464 or the #454 comments. - The run logs (r22, v24, the 28.65 s log, casa-c) are not available: - /Volumes/GLENDENNING/casa-rs-vlass/issue-446/ now holds only data and masks; - the NAS preservation copy /Volumes/home/casa-rs-glendenning-preserve/GLENDENNING/casa-rs-vlass/issue-446/ holds only input tars (casa-reduced-clean, cf-cache*, data*, masks, recovery-references, t51…), no receipts/runs or recovery-candidates. - Everything below therefore rests on repository and issue text.

    Governance finding. - RC lines 530–569 still require that the single-field landmarks be preserved, and say a landmark capability may leave the lineage only when generalised, superseded on the same landmark, or "explicitly retired by Brian" (RC:563–568). - The capability was deleted twice: at the August cutover and by IF-2. - I found no retirement record in: - #445, #454, #486 or #487 (bodies and comments, searched for landmark, 28.65, retire, response cache and preservation); - the cutover migration matrix; - HEAD docs. - RC at HEAD is therefore stale against the code.

    Where the wall time went. - casa-d: an initial grid of about 120 s (M5) plus about 9.5 s for each of 11 Metal residual majors (M3). - Today's CPU W1 pass is about 41 s per SPW, so a 4-SPW pass is roughly 165 s. That figure confounds three things: - GPU against CPU; - multi-threaded against W1; - precompiled per-sample programs (M2/M3) against on-the-fly preparation. - What it is not is I/O avoidance. #625's MS reads are cheap: the density-pass reads take 0.6 s (#659 comment 6207741158). - The single field's 126× came mostly from M1, which removed about 160 of 171 exact passes, together with M7, M8 and M10, which cut the per-cycle fixed costs.

    3. Fate of each mechanism¶

    Mechanism August → #486 #486 → IF Stated reason
    M1 response cache Deleted at 27b9b602fe and never ported Not present; never discussed by name The August cutover's transfer ratchet. RC:563–568 forbade silent retirement of landmark capabilities; I found no record of an owner retirement approval for the cutover. The #659 catalogue rejects "an image-domain residual (dirty image minus gridded model)" because it "cancels nearly equal f32 grids and breaks CASA's interpolate-then-subtract order" (comment 6134710065). That is a different construction, but the same argument would be raised.
    M2 compact replay, M3 Metal program, M4 grouped Deleted at 27b9b602fe; its conceptual successor (a different design, not a port) was the gridded-normal replay (gridded_normal_operator*, complete_data_operator*, metal_normal.rs) Deleted by IF-2 e7d29c8e5b under D1 (FP:71–87; deletion row FP:1025). Reaffirmed at R3 ("D1's re-traversal stands, and nothing argues for a replay cache", FP:1593) and at R4 ("the MAIN-walk profile does not justify a visibility or replay cache", FP:1695). D1: "not scientifically required"; about 40 B per row-channel, an 86 GB Wave3 reservation against a 36 GB input; valid only while weighting is frozen; forms the residual as a difference of two f64 images. ADR-0016 decision 2 (lines 38–41) and alternative 1 (lines 113–115). §5.9 outcome: "a tap-plan, record or placement cache across majors was rejected by design (D1, R3)" (FP:972–976). The #648 results say it was "not measured", and give deep32 cube sizes (2.6–8.9 GB a major).

    4. Assessment: what could come back, and what it might buy¶

    Constraints throughout. - One measurement operator and one pass (ADR-0016 decisions 1 and 2). - admit() before any allocation (D5). - No env vars in library crates (D9 / ADR-0016 decision 9). - Device code only in casa-imaging-metal (ADR-0016 decision 10). - The 16 GiB native policy. - #625's own bar is serial W1. Report serial gains and parallel or GPU gains separately. - The estimates below are mine. None of them is measured.

    1. AW kernel set on Metal (#653). This is the largest lever for the casa-d/#625 shape.
    2. August data: about 9.5 s per residual major for 6.4 M samples on the M4 GPU, against an estimated 165 s at CPU W1 today.
    3. It fits the existing GridBackend/Work::NativeResidualGrid design; no ADR change is needed.
    4. Risks:
      • D2's plain f32 atomics. August needed compensated accumulation (M6), and an f32 Metal residual "failed scientific comparison decisively" at 12,150² (salvage catalog, exact-metal-direct-cf-f32-residual).
      • Wide-support atomics contention: the R2 measurement #653 asked for.
      • Host preparation becomes the bottleneck. The 08-07 initial pass spent 155 s on source preparation against 136 s on Metal.
    5. It needs T0/T1 Metal AW laws and possibly an owner refinement of D2 (compensated f32).
    6. Plausible result: residual passes at August's 65–132 s plus an initial pass at August's best extrapolated figure (about 480–530 s) give about 870–1,320 s, which is 7–10×.
    7. 10× needs the initial pass to fall well below that too, for example a Metal initial grid without the CF thrash August hit. That is unmeasured.
    8. If only the residual passes move, the cap is about 7.5× (see the budget below).
    9. Multi-worker CPU AW.
    10. This already exists through Partition::Regions. #625 was timed only at W1.
    11. It is low conflict: measure W4 and charge the per-holder CF LRU (AwCatalog::charge_bytes).
    12. Plausible: 2.5–3.5× per pass, so #625 at about 1,400–1,900 s, roughly 5–6×. It does not count toward the serial bar.
    13. A bounded tap-plan (placement) cache.
    14. What it holds: per-sample CF key, placement and pointing phasor, without taps or values.
    15. ADR-0016 decision 2 explicitly permits "a bounded tap-plan cache … as a measured execution strategy". The FP §5.9 and #648 records still list it as rejected by design, so reopening it is an owner (Ask First) decision.
    16. Size: about 25 M samples on #625 at 16–32 B is 0.4–0.8 GB. It fits WeightCache-style admission.
    17. Gain: about 10–30% per pass on the CPU. Gridding dominates, and the per-row pointing conversion is about 10 s a pass. On Metal it would be much larger, because it removes the host-preparation bottleneck.
    18. A full record or program replay (observed V plus plans, as in M3/M4).
    19. D1's three objections apply unevenly to the August per-sample design:
      • "Residual as a difference of two f64 images": this described the #486 gridded-normal operator. The August Metal program formed V − A·m per sample; AwProjectMetalPredictionSample holds the observed values (L:11557). It therefore kept CASA's interpolate-then-subtract order.
      • "Valid only while weighting is frozen": this is already accepted for WeightCache (deviation 7).
      • Memory: this depends on workload and representation. Wave3 needed 86 GB at 40 B per sample. #625 at the #486 AW record size of 96 B per row-channel would be about 4 GB (about 42 M row-channels; 25.0 M accepted samples in r22). The measured M3/M4 representations were heavier: 9.3–15.2 GB resident and 17.8–19.8 GB footprint for the 16-SPW 63-field residual (PR #461).
    20. It remains Ask First, because ADR-0016 alternative 1 rejects it, so the ADR would have to be edited.
    21. ADR-0016 decision 2 is internally in tension. It says "no replay cache of visibility-independent factors" yet allows "a bounded tap-plan cache", which is exactly a cache of visibility-independent factors. The owner should resolve that wording before item 3 or item 4 is attempted.
    22. Spilled replay was slower: 14.3 GB reread a refresh made it 2× slower than resident (HP:8000–8006).
    23. Worth it only after item 1, and only if host preparation then dominates.
    24. The frozen-base response (M1), generalised to a bounded set of delta positions with exact shadow cycles and fallback (RC:570–585; HP:3405–3414 oracle requirements: dyadic calibration, a held-out exact shadow cycle, coefficient trust regions, decision-margin certificates, exact fallback).
    25. It is not named in D1, and it is mathematically exact for the linear H up to rounding.
    26. It lives in the runtime's major/minor loop, and calls run_major_cycle for the calibration columns.
    27. Cost: 512 MiB per position at 4096² and about 4.4 GiB at 12,150², so only a few positions can be admitted.
    28. Buys a lot on many-cycle single-field rows: 171 → 11 exact passes. Little on #625 (7 majors) or casa-d (11 majors), whose model delta is not single-position.
    29. It needs owner sign-off against the #659 "image-domain residual" rejection.
    30. Sparse MT-MFS RHS (M7), radix MADFM and cached n-sigma (M8).
    31. These are solver-internal (Solver::State, PlaneStatistics) with no architectural conflict.
    32. They matter only on 171-cycle rows, worth seconds per cycle; they are negligible on #625.
    33. M5 and M10. Today's Regions partition plus NEON FFTW cover most of them. Parallel per-term FFTs are a small fixed-cost win on many-cycle rows.

    The 10× budget for #625, using 16-SPW data where it exists: - 10× on #625 is about 907 s in total. Today: initial pass 603 s plus six residual passes of 651–719 s, all at CPU W1. - Initial grid. - The best August initial grid was the multi-worker sparse CPU path, 120–133 s at 4 SPWs on 63 fields. - Scaled by SPW count to 16 SPWs, that is about 480–530 s. This is an estimate: the per-SPW cost was roughly constant today, 41 s per SPW per pass. - The 16-SPW single-field v59 initial grid was 60.5 s, but that is 1/63 of the rows. - Residual passes. The only 16-SPW all-field residual figures are 132.34 s (incumbent) and 65.17 s (grouped). Both stop before FFT and normalisation. - Total. About 480–530 + 6 × (65–132) ≈ 870–1,320 s. The best August mechanisms therefore extrapolate to roughly the 10× line, or short of it, on #625. They were never run there, and the resident replay footprint is 17.8–19.8 GB. - The initial grid is as large a lever as the residual. Today's 603 s initial pass alone uses two-thirds of the 907 s budget. Accelerating only the residual majors, to about 100 s each, caps #625 near 9,074 / (603 + 600) ≈ 7.5×.

    No row passes 10× on the serial CPU alone. The single-field 4-SPW row is likely bound by its roughly 172 passes of FFT and normalisation fixed cost. It has not been measured on today's build, and its CASA time is the mini's.

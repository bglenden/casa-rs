Counter-based Gaussian noise for visibility cells: σ times a standard normal added to every real and imaginary part of a cell, each draw a pure function of a stream key and the sample’s place in the data.

§A draw

A draw is Philox4x32-10 (philox4x32_10; Salmon, Moraes, Dror and Shaw, SC11) of a 128-bit counter under a 64-bit key: four 32-bit words. The counter is the row (its low word, then its high word), the channel and the correlation pair (correlations 0 and 1 are pair 0, 2 and 3 pair 1). Words 0 and 1 make correlation 2·pair’s (real, imaginary) normals, words 2 and 3 correlation 2·pair + 1’s. The key names the stream and belongs to the caller: casa-simulation, for one, keys thermal noise by the term’s seed and how many times the term has been applied. Distinct keys give independent streams, and one key the same values every time.

Philox keeps no state, so the noise of a sample depends only on (key, row, channel, correlation): it is bitwise the same in any order, for any batch size, on any number of workers and on any backend that computes the same arithmetic.

Normals come from Box–Muller on a pair of words (box_muller): with u₁ = (w₀ + 1)·2⁻³² in (0, 1] and θ = 2π·w₁·2⁻³², the pair √(−2 ln u₁)·(cos θ, sin θ) is two independent standard normals. They are computed in f32, with the logarithm, sine and cosine evaluated on the words themselves rather than by the platform’s libm: only IEEE-754 additions, multiplications, one division and a square root, all correctly rounded and none fused, so a draw is bitwise the same on every platform. The noise added is (r·σ)·cos θ and (r·σ)·sin θ (scaled_draw): scaling the radius once saves a multiplication per sample over scaling each normal, with the same number of roundings.

§Accuracy

The draws replace the double-precision draws the same words gave before (#855; the tests keep that code as an oracle). With ε = 2⁻²⁴, the unit roundoff of f32, the largest errors against it over all 2³² words are 2.003ε for the radius and 2.892ε for the sine and the cosine, relatively. The radius depends on the first word only and the angle on the second only, so a standard normal, rounded once more, is within 2.003ε + 2.892ε + ε < 6ε of the double-precision normal, relatively, for every pair of words. Over 2³³ normals the largest error is 5.18ε, and 56 % of them are the double-precision normal rounded to f32. Scaling by σ, as (r·σ)·cos θ rather than (r·cos θ)·σ, adds one rounding. Against noise of standard deviation σ the difference is of order 10⁻⁷σ, below what an f32 visibility resolves.

Each bound is relative down to the smallest values, because each part is computed from exact integers where precision matters: near u₁ = 1 the logarithm takes 1 − u₁ as an integer, and the angle within its octant is taken from the word’s low bits. The largest draw is √(64 ln 2) ≈ 6.66 standard deviations (6.660437, that value correctly rounded to f32, at w₀ = 0); an exact normal exceeds it about once in 4·10⁹.

§Drawing channels together

A row’s draws are made sixteen channels at a time: the counters of one correlation pair in consecutive channels differ only in their channel word, so the generator and Box–Muller run on all of them in step, in vector registers (Philox’s multiplies with NEON intrinsics on aarch64). Every lane performs exactly the operations of a draw made alone, in the same order and with nothing fused, so the batching changes the speed and not one bit of the noise. The tests check this against the same draws made one at a time (philox4x32_10 and scaled_draw), check both against the double-precision oracle within the bounds above, and hold a few draws and one cell to known bits, so that a platform that rounds differently fails.

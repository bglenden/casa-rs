    MSv2/MSv4 trial evidence¶

    Status: revision 4 (the walkthrough's decisions, the cleanups revision 2 exposed, and the answers to the reviews of revision 3); the read contract frozen narrowly for M2 by the owner, who waived a re-review (2026-10-25); the write-back and converter steps added (2026-10-26); not M0/M1 acceptance Truth class: trial evidence Date: 2026-10-26 (converter section) Handoff: trial-handoff.md Review brief: review-brief.md

    This is the evidence index for the trial's first assignment: two worked examples (A, dirty-image channel planes; B, a gain solve) and the early bidirectional XRADIO check. It records what ran, with what, and what it showed, including what is missing. Nothing here claims M0 or M1 complete: the full caller and Measures inventory, every Measures family's bulk route, general updates and creation, failure injection and the benchmark matrix remain later requirements.

    Revision 4: the reviews of revision 3¶

    Astra's design review and Fable's independent code review of revision 3 (a6852cc) on bglenden/casa-rs#898 found three blocking and four should-fix design points (Astra: B1–B3, S1–S4) and two blocking, seven should-fix and seven minor code points (Fable). Revision 4 answers every one; the design documents now state the current contract directly (contracts, revision 7).

    The owner's decisions on revision 4¶

    • No re-review. The owner waived an independent re-review of revision 4: what remains open is implementation, to be found as the applications are ported, not design.
    • A narrow freeze. The read contract, contracts sections 1–6 and creation from batches, is frozen for M2; updates and the provisional capabilities (its section 8) are not. Porting the applications is expected to show where the design is not sufficient, and such a finding reopens the contract concerned under the implementation plan's change control.
    • Whole-chunk writes. Of counting partial chunks, refusing unaligned batches, or holding partial chunks in the writer, the owner chose holding (the Fable row above).

    Revision 2's own review (this document's "What revision 2 taught", the scale check below, and the owner's questions on performance and memory) found places where the boundary was right in substance but awkward or unsafe in form. Revision 3 changes them, and nothing else; the examples, the interoperability checks and every adapter law are unchanged in what they assert.

    Revision 2: the design walkthrough's decisions, implemented¶

    After revision 1 the owner walked through the design chunk by chunk. The 25 decisions, the principle behind them (MSv4 is the future; MSv2 is assembled into its structures), the vocabulary (batch shape, traversal order, payload order; now in GLOSSARY.md), the facts verified against the pinned XRADIO source and the implementation order are recorded in revision-2-design.md, whose last section says what the implementation did with each decision. Revision 2 implemented them all, with the qualifications listed there (an MSv2 creator from batches was not built). D18 was completed once ADR-0016 was amended to let imaging depend on the definitional crates: both adapters, imaging and the simulator share one Correlation of all 32 casacore Stokes types. Every open question listed at the end of this document is resolved. Both examples run unchanged in their science: the planes and the gains are bit-identical to production from both stores.

    What revision 2 taught¶

    • The grid removes code everywhere it reaches. Deleting Irregular removed every topology match from the consumers, the writer's scatter path and the "rows not in time order" refusal; the MSv2 cursor's only new code is the placement of present rows (and padding) when a time is incomplete or a correlation subset is delivered. The complete, all-product case still reads straight into the batch arrays.
    • Linking to the catalogue removed a class of disagreement. Two writer refusal tests (reversed correlation labels, a shifted frequency in a batch) became impossible to write: a batch can no longer carry those values, so the writer checks only what a batch states (its times, intervals, baseline table) and what the declaration states (correlations, units).
    • A byte budget changes a consumer's bookkeeping. The imaging source sized its maximum block from the full window and the pass's own assertion caught the narrower one-plane wave fitting more times; the block is now sized from the narrowest window's estimate. The estimate made that a one-line fix, which is what D10 is for.
    • Asking before lending (next_owned(None)) removed the extra buffer wait at the end of a traversal from both producers; the work-unit probe and the concurrent solve ask first and lend only for a batch.
    • The estimate equals the realization by construction, since binding computes it once and the cursors carry it; the test asserts equality rather than re-deriving it.
    • Subset gathering costs a copy on both adapters (MSv4 decodes the full correlation axis into scratch; MSv2 reads full cells), counted in copied_elements. A strided in-place decode per product is the next measurement, not a design question.
    • The dependency policy bounded vocabulary unification, until it was amended. casa-types owns Correlation and MUvw, but ADR-0016 let casa-imaging-model depend on no workspace crate, so the model kept CorrelationType and private copies of other measure vocabulary. The amendment of 2026-10-25 lets every imaging crate use the definitional crates; the model now uses casa_types::Correlation. Moving the model to the shared vocabulary exposed that revision 2's eight-product Correlation was narrower than MSv4, which allows all 32 Stokes types. The model's DirectionFrame, FrequencyFrame and Epoch remain its own and are the next candidates.

    Fixtures¶

    Both fixtures describe the same observation and live in crates/casa-ms/tests/fixtures/ms_trial/ (53 KB and 43 KB compressed), with crates/casa-ms/tests/fixtures/ms_trial/manifest.json recording the oracle versions, the true gains and every array's shape, chunks, codecs and fill value as XRADIO wrote them.

    • MSv2 ms_trial.ms. Written by the casa-rs synthetic simulator (crates/casa-ms/examples/ms_trial_fixture.rs): five VLA-A antennas (ten baselines, no autocorrelations), one field, twelve 10 s integrations, eight 128 MHz Q-band channels in LSRK and four linear correlations. The sky is a 1 Jy point at the phase centre and a 0.1 Jy point 14 × 9 cells away, 8 % Q and −5 % U so the cross hands carry signal. Then tools/ms-trial/make_fixtures.py (casacore through arcae) adds seeded complex noise (σ = 0.02 Jy per component) and known per-antenna, per-receptor gains that change every 60 s (two solution intervals); sets WEIGHT varying by row and correlation (SIGMA = 1/√WEIGHT) and 62 element flags of 3,840 (one channel, single cross hands, a whole correlation, a whole row, the last channel of four rows); and writes UVW's MEASINFO reference ITRF. FLAG_ROW stays false, because XRADIO does not carry it.
    • MSv4 ms_trial.ps.zarr. XRADIO's convert_msv2_to_processing_set of that MSv2 with partition_scheme=[], main_chunksize={"time": 5, "frequency": 4} (three time chunks, the last a ragged edge, by two frequency chunks), default Blosc-LZ4 for the data variables and Zstandard for coordinates, and with_pointing=False. XRADIO's own check_datatree reports no schema issues.

    Regenerate both with tools/ms-trial/make_fixtures.sh (needs cargo and uv). The payload is identical on every run; XRADIO's creation dates in the metadata are not. Origin and licence: generated for this repository, no external data.

    Fixture findings¶

    • casa-rs's synthetic writer leaves UVW's MEASINFO without Ref; XRADIO then writes frame: null and fails its own schema. The fixture script writes Ref: ITRF, as CASA does.
    • XRADIO's pointing dataset types time_pointing as time, which its own schema rejects; the fixture omits pointing.
    • The synthetic writer's corruption panics for four correlations (it indexes antenna gains by correlation, for at most two), so noise and gains are applied by the fixture script.
    • XRADIO relabels MSv2's UVW ITRF as the phase centre's frame (fk5), following CASA fixvis's note that the label is usually wrong; names antennas NAME_STATION and fields NAME_FIELDID; and declares the visibility unit as "unkown".

    Adapter laws¶

    crates/casa-ms/tests/observation_io.rs and its modules refusals, writer and work: 28 tests, all passing (the writer laws are described under interoperability, the refusals in revision 1's table and revision 4's). The shared types have 12 unit tests of their own (cargo test -p casa-observation), and the adapters' group index, padding spread, SCHEDULE parser and partial-chunk count 7.

    • Both catalogues describe the same observation in each store's own names (antennas with equal positions, field, phase centre, scan 1 with its intent, one observing session, one partition of the same content: frequencies and widths, LSRK, correlation order, intent; data-group roles, units and UVW frames: effective J2000 in both, stored ITRF in MSv2; the MSv2 partition keys its feed pair, the MSv4 one has none), and bind to each other by content.
    • Every sample XRADIO wrote equals its MSv2 row exactly: visibilities, flags, weights (MSv2's per-correlation WEIGHT seen through every channel, MSv4's spectral WEIGHT), UVW, time, time centroid, interval, exposure and scan. This holds for the whole band and for channel windows 2–5 and 7, at 1, 2, 5 and 12 whole times per v4 batch and 1, 3 and 5 per v2 batch. The v4 store holds exactly the 120 v2 acquisitions.
    • The v2 batch's time auxiliaries equal MAIN's TIME_CENTROID, INTERVAL, EXPOSURE and SCAN_NUMBER as the production typed column accessors read them cell by cell, each sample finding its row by time and antennas (the grid, not MAIN's row order, orders the batch).
    • Both stores deliver one batch shape: 4 times × 10 baselines under a four-time budget, field and scan per time, frequencies and baselines borrowed from the catalogue, the [40, 8, 4] value view the decode buffer itself. The stores differ only in sharing and strides: MSv2 WEIGHT has strides [4, 0, 1] and its interval is per sample; MSv4 weights are standard layout and its interval one constant.
    • MSv4 Unix-second times re-express the MSv2 MJD-second times exactly.
    • 60 s groups keyed by field and scan: each bin's 60 samples arrive once, in order, whatever the batch size (1–12 times). Closure is announced exactly once, either as a Last/Whole fragment or, from a store opened to defer it, as a later GroupEnd.
    • A correlation subset (These([YY, XX])) is delivered as [XX, YY] with the values and weights of those products and flags either their own or the OR over all four, from both stores; the fixture flags some cross hands alone, so the two policies differ on it.
    • One time that does not fit the byte budget fails binding; a traversal read through a pool of one buffer gets each batch in that buffer, has it back whenever the batch is dropped, and takes none at the end (revision 3; revision 2 asked without buffers, NeedBuffers).
    • An MSv2 row removed from the fixture leaves a padded cell: flagged, NaN values, weights, UVW and auxiliaries (weight 0 until the write-back trial), excluded by the validity rule; the other 119 samples are unchanged, the batch count is the grid's and the estimate reports the padded fraction (1/120). A duplicated row is refused at opening, naming both rows. A row with FLAG_ROW set has every value flagged in the batch, its values untouched, and writes to MSv4 without refusal.
    • A grouped batch split into two channel windows on two threads, one held back, completes each group's input once and finalizes each group once, after both windows' work; per-group sums equal a sequential traversal's, from both stores.
    • The estimate models read amplification from the chunk grid before reading: 15/12 for the whole band (Zarr stores the ragged last time chunk at full size), and twice that for a two-channel window of four-channel chunks; it reports the natural blocks (5 times × 4 channels for MSv4, the tile shape for MSv2), the bytes per sample of the roles (352 for the MSv2 batch with its per-correlation weight and three per-sample scalars, 456 for the MSv4 batch) and the padded fraction. Binding returns the same estimate, and the cursor's count of touched chunks, by the same model, matches.

The payload variables' fill values are the padding convention (flag true, values and weights NaN, as XRADIO pads). Chunks are regular (here [5, 10, 4, 4]). Payload variables use Blosc-LZ4, none or Zstandard; coordinates use Zstandard. Each writer creates its own staging directory beside the destination (exclusively, under a name no other writer or later retry uses) and publishes with a rename that refuses to replace (renameat2(RENAME_NOREPLACE) on Linux, renamex_np(RENAME_EXCL) on macOS, a checked rename elsewhere). It writes pointing only for the converter ("Geometry inputs"), and no system-calibration or weather datasets, EFFECTIVE_CHANNEL_WIDTH, or consolidated metadata, so XRADIO warns that it fell back to non-consolidated reads. It writes no dataset larger than one partition, only unix/utc times, and offers no update, append or resize.

    Conversion conventions applied explicitly. XRADIO names antennas NAME_STATION, fields and spectral windows NAME_ID, and an absent source Unknown, and spells undeclared visibility units "unkown". The trial's MSv2 → MSv4 conversion renames antennas, fields and the spectral window in its copy of the catalogue, as a stated policy, so the stores compare equal. It does not relabel UVW: the MSv2 adapter already resolved MSv2's ITRF-labelled UVW to the phase centres' J2000, which the writer checks each batch against. Scans and intents come from the batch and the catalogue.

    Interop findings¶

    • zarrs writes a configuration-less codec as a bare name. zarr-python 3.4.1 refuses to open it (Expected dict, got str), so the writer always gives the bytes codec its endian. zarrs also adds a _zarrs provenance attribute unless include_zarrs_metadata(false) is set per call; the writer sets it.
    • Zarr stores edge chunks at full size. Reading the twelve times of five-time chunks decodes fifteen (amplification 1.25). Writing batches that straddle chunk boundaries makes zarrs read, modify and rewrite the partial chunks in place. Revision 4's writer holds a batch's times in such a chunk until the chunk is complete, so creation in time order encodes each chunk once; an update that covers a chunk partly still reads, modifies and rewrites it, which is not the contract's atomic object replacement.
    • XRADIO's own outputs need care. Without a UVW reference it writes frame: null; its pointing dataset fails its own schema; and it writes visibility units "unkown".
    • unix times are UTC-based in every scale. XRADIO defines its time attributes by astropy, whose unix format counts from 1970-01-01 UTC whatever the scale: Time(1700000000., format="unix", scale="tai") is 22:13:57 TAI, while "Unix epoch in TAI plus the offset" gives 22:13:20. The first pass made exactly that error for tai; revision 1 reads and writes unix/utc only, and the oracle now compares instants.

    Write-back: update sessions on both stores¶

    Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-26 as the step before any consumer is converted: the update side of the design is the part most likely to push back on the frozen read contract, and nearly every consumer writes something (MODEL_DATA, CORRECTED_DATA, FLAG). It realizes, narrowly, the update obligations of contracts.md section 7 that the design reviews raised: mutation scope over broadcast and data-group aliases (Pro R1), the failure scope of an interrupted update and MSv4 atomic replacement (Pro R3, Fable), the in-process lock rule of a mixed read/update session (Fable), no read-back of a unit a patch supplies whole (Fable), and what a flag change writes (Fable).

    The interface (casa_observation::update, casa_ms::observation_io::update):

    • open_update(path, options, targets) takes this process's write intent on the store, opens it once, and resolves the declared UpdateTargets (data group, role, alias scope, create) before any payload. The session lends the observation for reading and Updaters for writing, each bound to a traversal and some targets; live updaters claim disjoint times of each variable (time selection, "Updaters of a split").
    • Updater::write(batch, values) takes one PatchValues per bound target for a batch the session delivered: visibilities (with a mask of the elements written, since imaging port step 5), a flag change (set and clear masks; elements neither names keep their stored flag), or weights for the delivered channels or all of them. Values are checked against the batch before anything is written. Updater::write_at(address, values) writes against the batch's owned address once its buffers are gone, for every change but those whose check reads the payload.
    • The batch's sample addresses are found from its own times and the partition's baselines (the MSv2 grid's rows, the MSv4 dataset's time index): the frozen read contract needed no change to be written back.

    a_shared_variable_is_written_only_with_its_aliases_accepted base and model share FLAG: a flag target of either is refused unless it accepts the other group as an alias, which the resolved target names; so is a base flag target in a session that creates model.
    flag_changes_keep_row_flags_and_padding Clearing FLAG leaves a FLAG_ROW row excluded; a padded cell (no MSv2 row) receives nothing (unaddressed) and clearing its flag is refused.
    msv4_padding_stays_flagged Clearing a flag or writing a finite weight where the MSv4 store holds padding (a non-finite weight) is refused, and so is clearing flags from a traversal that does not read weights.
    padding_the_writer_stores_stays_padding An MSv2 with a row removed, written by casa-rs's writer, holds its padding with a NaN weight, as XRADIO does: the updater refuses to clear its flag or weight it, and it reads back flagged.
    an_empty_flag_change_keeps_every_flag A flag change with neither mask keeps every flag: MSv2 rewrites the cells as stored, MSv4 writes nothing.
    clearing_every_msv4_flag_erases_chunks_and_finishes Clearing every flag makes each FLAG chunk equal to the fill value, which zarrs erases rather than stores, an absent chunk included; the update finishes (its directories synchronized) and reads back unflagged.
    an_msv4_batch_of_other_channels_is_refused_whole A batch whose channels are not the updater's traversal's is refused before any target takes it, so nothing of it is written.
    a_session_reads_what_its_updaters_wrote A second traversal of the session's observation delivers what its first updater wrote, in both stores.
    one_update_session_per_store_and_one_updater_per_time A second update session on a store, or a second live updater of a session over the same times, is a Conflict; one variable bound twice to an updater is refused (whichever wrote last would win); read-only observations open beside them. MSv2 states casacore's cross-process lock; MSv4 states atomic units and no cross-process lock.
    an_updater_writes_any_of_its_batches_by_address Imaging port step 5 (#932): an updater takes its traversal's batches in any order and any of them, written against their addresses once their buffers hold later batches, each under a mask; the elements a mask writes hold the patch, every other element (the batch left out included) keeps what it held, and MSv2 neither reads nor writes a sample its mask leaves wholly.     a_failed_first_write_leaves_an_absent_chunk_absent FLAG's chunk c/2/0/0/0 is absent (equal to the fill value); its failed first write leaves it absent, reading as the fill value, and the four chunks before it replaced.
    a_failure_before_writing_changes_nothing A failure before any byte is written changes nothing.

    How each store writes.

    • MSv2 reuses production's bounded selected-row mutation (MeasurementSetWriteSession), one per batch over its present rows, under casacore's write lock on MAIN, which the session holds from its first live updater to its last and takes with its handle reopened, so a merge never reads a table older than the lock. Each batch's cells are written in place, a cell read back only when a patch covers it partly. A failure leaves the rows already written, as casacore's writes do.
    • MSv4 holds one chunk-row of times per target variable (the window), over the channel chunks the traversal reaches (every channel for weights), and replaces each chunk whole through a store whose writes go to a synchronized temporary file renamed over the chunk. The window counts the elements assigned in each chunk: a chunk assigned whole is encoded without reading it, one assigned partly is read once per window, and an untouched one costs nothing. Every replacement is atomic; there is no transaction across chunks. Finishing synchronizes the directories the replacements changed (those whose objects it renamed or removed), so a finished update is on disk wherever the filesystem honours fsync.

    What it shows about the design.

    1. Addressing is enough. A batch's times and the partition's baselines locate every sample in both stores; no update map had to be added to the read side.
    2. Occupancy is implicit. Padding is a missing MSv2 row or a NaN MSv4 weight; the batch carries no presence mask, so the MSv4 updater needs weights read to keep padding flagged. An explicit presence role in the batch (contracts.md section 8, "Occupancy") would make this uniform.
    3. Production's MSv2 mutation plans its rows up front and takes values as one ArrayValue per cell. The updater now plans per batch, one mutation per batch under the lock the session holds, so the mutation no longer needs the traversal planned whole; skipping unchanged batches would still need the updater to accept gaps (it takes every batch in order), and the M2 hot path still needs typed columns.
    4. MSv4 cannot gain a data group. Writing a model to an MSv4 store needs a structural write (a new variable and group); MSv2 adds the column.
    5. Data groups are now the stores' own. The MSv2 adapter lists corrected and model where CORRECTED_DATA and MODEL_DATA exist, as XRADIO's converter names them, all sharing FLAG, the weights and UVW.
    6. Padding needed one mark. The MSv2 adapter padded with weight 0 and casa-rs's writer stored that, so an MSv4 written from a padded MSv2 could not tell padding from a flagged acquisition, and a flag pass could clear it. Padding is now a NaN weight in both stores (as XRADIO pads), a contract revision to section 3; the validity rule already excluded a non-finite weight. Finding 2's presence mask would remove the inference.
    7. A new model starts as CASA's. The first version left a created MODEL_DATA holding DATA wherever the pass did not reach, which gives a later solve identity gains there. CASA initializes a new MODEL_DATA to a unit point source, 1 and 0 in the cross hands RL, LR, XY and YX (VisSetUtil::initScrCols, called by tclean's addScrCols and by VisSet), and CORRECTED_DATA from DATA. The owner chose CASA's model (2026-10-26): the updater installs MODEL_DATA from DATA's storage, the layout applycal's CORRECTED_DATA uses, and overwrites every row with that model before the pass writes. One helper, casa_ms::initial_model_visibility, holds the rule, and every casa-rs writer that adds MODEL_DATA uses it: this updater, imaging's in-place writer (which filled a new column with 0 until this change) and the VLA importer. The simulator's predict writes 1 in every product, cross hands included, because that is what CASA's Simulator::predict does after the prediction (vb.setModelVisCube(Complex(1.0,0.0))); it overwrites the model rather than initializing a new column. Every path that adds MODEL_DATA or CORRECTED_DATA to an existing MeasurementSet (this updater, imaging's writer, applycal) first clears the cached sort keywords, as CASA's addScrCols does, and every path that writes MODEL_DATA (this updater and imaging's writer), created or not, first clears every field's virtual model, as CASA's imagers do, since CASA would otherwise read it in the column's place. Both go through casa_ms::scratch_columns (#912).

    Not done: per-batch MSv2 row planning; WEIGHT_SPECTRUM and FLAG_CATEGORY (the fixture has neither); MSv4 structural writes; a process actually killed during a replacement (the injected failure leaves what a kill leaves); sweeping stray temporary files; the MSv2 read-back of partly covered cells one cell at a time; a failure while MODEL_DATA is being seeded, which leaves the rows not yet seeded holding DATA. The cost of synchronizing each replaced chunk at scale is measured in the converter section (finding 4).

    Independent review. A separate agent reviewed the first version and found three blocking defects, all fixed with laws: a flag change with neither mask cleared every MSv2 flag of a whole cell; MSv4 padding written by casa-rs's writer (weight 0) could be cleared or weighted into an acquisition; and MODEL_DATA was seeded from DATA (finding 7). It also asked for one binding per variable, aliases for groups the session creates, a directory synchronization at finish and a window limited to the channels reached, all done. Its check of the fixes found one more defect (finishing failed after an absent chunk was erased, since its directory did not exist) and a batch of other channels taken by one target before another refused it; both are fixed with laws.

    Converter: MSv2 ↔ MSv4 through the boundary¶

    Step (3) of the order agreed with the owner on 2026-10-26: a converter in both directions, with MSv2 creation, the conversion-loss policy, and the retention, codec-thread, pin and synchronization measurements on real data. Both directions read the source as any consumer reads it (catalogue, grids, batches) and create the destination from those alone.

    The interface (casa_ms::observation_io::convert):

    • to_msv4(source, destination, options) and to_msv2(…) take either store. ConversionOptions names the data groups (default every group, base first), the accepted losses, the batch budget, MSv4 workers, chunks, codec and provenance. A ConversionReport returns the losses taken, partitions, batches, samples, and the MSv2 rows or MSv4 elements written.
    • Losses are inventoried and refused before anything is written. A Loss names what the destination cannot hold and its LossKind (Sigma, RowFlags, FlagCategories, DataGroups, Tables, Columns) is what a caller accepts:
    • from an MSv2: SIGMA/SIGMA_SPECTRUM, any FLAG_ROW set, held FLAG_CATEGORY cells (every row is read, 256 at a time, so at most about 100 MB of held cells at once; that costs about 60 ms per 2 million rows over reading 4,096 at a time, as measured on a simulated MS of 2,016,000 rows), MAIN columns outside the boundary's roles, an ARRAY_ID or PROCESSOR_ID above 0 (a negative id names nothing: casacore's UVFITS filler writes PROCESSOR_ID −1), the WEIGHT summary beside WEIGHT_SPECTRUM, subtables the catalogue does not carry (system calibration, weather, flag commands, history, Doppler, frequency offsets, sources, and any further table a keyword names), the POINTING content the destination does not hold (planned before writing: "Geometry inputs"), FEED receptors that vary in time (the last row is kept), a PROCESSOR of more than one row or of one that is not a correlator, and STATE rows that say more than an intent (a sub-scan, calibration or load, a reference or non-signal row);
    • from an MSv4: sub-datasets other than antennas, fields and pointing, and variables the writer does not write;
    • in either, unselected data groups;
    • into an MSv4, antennas no partition's baselines name (Loss::UnusedAntennas, below);
    • into an MSv2, SIGMA itself (Loss::SigmaDerived, below).

    Columns of the subtables the catalogue does carry (an antenna's offset, a field's delay direction) are not inventoried. Fields whose direction moves are refused in either direction: those that follow an ephemeris (EPHEMERIS_ID ≥ 0) and those whose direction is a polynomial in time (NUM_POLY > 0, which XRADIO refuses too), since the catalogue would hold an offset or a constant term as the phase centre. Into an MSv4 only the fields MAIN's rows name are checked, as XRADIO checks the fields it converts; into an MSv2, which writes every FIELD row, every row is. - Lockstep groups. A partition's selected groups are read by one traversal each, sized to the same whole times per batch (the first with every role, the others their visibilities only), so a destination writes every variable of a time range together (convert/lockstep.rs). Batches align to the destination's chunk or, for an MSv2, to the source's natural time block.

    To MSv4. One dataset per partition in one processing set (Msv4ProcessingSetWriter: one staging directory, datasets written concurrently, published by one rename that refuses to replace). Further groups are VISIBILITY_<GROUP> variables beside one FLAG, WEIGHT and UVW. Each dataset's antenna_xds lists the antennas its baselines name, as XRADIO writes it, and an antenna no partition names is reported (Loss::UnusedAntennas); relocatable_antennas is set only for the telescopes whose antennas XRADIO renames, by the dataset's telescope as XRADIO sets it (names follow the first session's telescope, below, so in an MSv2 whose sessions name different telescopes the two can disagree). Both converters list a dataset's antennas in the MSv2's ANTENNA order, so the MSv4 reader merges the datasets' lists keeping every one's order (a topological merge, the antenna first seen first among those free to come next): an MSv2 written back numbers its antennas as the original did whenever the datasets determine the order, XRADIO's stores included, and no baseline of either converter's store comes back reversed (both of a baseline's antennas are in one list). Each antenna_xds is read once, and checked there; opening 3,001 datasets took as long as before the merge (24.0–24.9 s against 24.2–24.8 s, a debug build; in a release build 4.89–5.42 s against 4.96–5.53 s, where reading every antenna_xds twice had taken 6.37–6.61 s), and each dataset's baselines resolve their antenna names by hash rather than by a scan of its antennas. An MSv4's datasets keep their names. An MSv2's datasets are named as XRADIO names them: the file name without .ms, then the partition's number in the order the partitions' first MAIN rows appear (XRADIO groups with sort=False), padded with zeros to the width of the largest (Msv2Observation::first_row). Its names are XRADIO's too: antennas NAME_STATION for the telescopes it renames (ALMA, VLA, NOEMA and EVLA, judged by the first observation's telescope where XRADIO judges each partition's; others keep their names), fields NAME_FIELDID, sources NAME_SOURCEID by the field's SOURCE_ID, and spectral windows NAME_SPWID by the partition's SPECTRAL_WINDOW row (spw_ID when unnamed), which the MSv2 adapter now reports (Msv2Observation::spectral_window_id). XRADIO names a source it finds no SOURCE row for in the window Unknown (or Unknown_SOURCEID in a partition of several fields); this converter names it from the field when another window's SOURCE row names it, and writes Unknown when none does. The MSv4 reader reads both placeholders as no source. Two partitionings differ from XRADIO's: the MSv2 adapter splits partitions by feed pair as well, and gives a row with STATE_ID −1 no intents where XRADIO gives it the last STATE row's (NumPy's index −1). The first makes more datasets here; the second can, or can give the same datasets other intents. Either way the datasets numbered after the first difference can hold other data than XRADIO's of the same name. Partitions are converted workers at a time.

    To MSv2. A new MeasurementSet through production's creation path:

    • subtables from the catalogue: an antenna and a feed per antenna, a field per field, a source per source in each spectral window, an observation per session whose TIME_RANGE spans its integrations edge to edge, polarizations whose products name receptors by their place in the feeds (which must agree), and spectral windows, polarizations, data descriptions and states shared by partitions of equal content (convert/msv2_tables.rs). A data description is shared only by partitions whose state or observation differs, since an MSv2 keys partitions by data description, state and observation and would read two alike back as one.
    • MAIN rows through the variable-shape creation writers (MeasurementSetWritePlan::variable_array_creation), one hypercube per visibility shape, partition after partition, in time and baseline order. base, corrected and model become DATA, CORRECTED_DATA and MODEL_DATA; another group name is refused.
    • Names are the MSv4's. An MSv4 made from an MSv2 holds XRADIO's names (above), so the MSv2 converted back from it says them: a round trip renames field trial to trial_0 and antenna W08 of a VLA to W08_W08, and a second round trip suffixes again, as XRADIO's conversion of the round-tripped MSv2 would. The converter writes what the MSv4 says rather than guessing which suffixes XRADIO added; a CASA script that selects by the original names needs the MSv4's (finding 8).
    • Padding, as decided in D23 (revision-2-design.md). A sample whose every weight is non-finite is padding (section 3's one mark), and an acquisition is everything else. By default every grid cell becomes a row, and a padded cell a row flagged throughout with NaN data, weights and UVW (FLAG_ROW stays false: its flags already exclude it, and a reader folding FLAG_ROW in reads the same); its keys stay valid (the grid's interval, exposure 0, time centroid at its time), so the MSv2 reopens, and it reads back, and converts back, as padding. A conversion that must reproduce an original sparse row set declares it (PaddingRows::Omitted): padding then becomes no row. An MSv2's row count is fixed before its rows are written and the batch carries no presence mask, so that policy first reads the first group's weights to count each partition's acquisitions (finding 5); the default takes the count from the grids.
    • SIGMA is derived as CASA's mstransform derives it when corrected data become data (weightToSigma): 1/√w, and −1 where w is not positive. This is reported as Loss::SigmaDerived and must be accepted, since no batch carries sigma; right for weights never recalibrated, wrong for the others (finding 6).
    • Refused before anything is written: times in a scale other than UTC, feeds other than 0, units that differ between partitions of one column, fields in a frame other than J2000, a scan without a casacore number, feeds whose receptors differ, a correlation product the feeds cannot name (a Stokes product, or a receptor they lack), a data group selected twice, a source with nothing to write. Weights that vary over channels (an MSv2 WEIGHT holds one per correlation and no WEIGHT_SPECTRUM is written) are refused by the counting pass when there is one, and otherwise when reached. The MSv2 is written by one thread into a staging directory renamed into place, and removed on failure, so a refusal publishes nothing.

    Findings.

    1. XRADIO names a spectral window by its MSv2 row, not by partition. The first to_msv4 suffixed each window with its partition's index, which equals XRADIO's SPECTRAL_WINDOW id only when every window is one partition; pilot.ms has two partitions per window. Fixed (above); the scale check above, which had called pilot.ms's 64 partitions 64 spectral windows, now says 32.
    2. "Concurrency target 1" was not one thread (E1). zarrs 0.23.14 encodes and decodes min(chunk_concurrent_minimum, chunks) chunks of one access at once on rayon's global pool whatever the target, and the minimum defaults to 4; building its options also starts that pool (one thread per core, 8 here). Every MSv4 read, write and update now uses one codec_options() with both set to 1, so a codec runs on the calling thread and parallelism is the caller's workers. The pool still starts, idle: one worker's process has 10 threads, four workers' 13, eight workers' 17 (an MSv2 read, 2), and one worker's three read passes spend 7.8 s of CPU in 7.9 s. The cost of no longer borrowing those threads: a one-worker read pass of the converted pilot.ms went from 1.75 s to 2.49–2.59 s and four workers from 0.72–0.77 s to 0.82–0.86 s; eight workers take 0.64 s; conversion with eight workers is unchanged and with one about 2 s slower (19.5–20.7 s before, with 10.4 s of CPU). In-partition parallelism comes back by giving workers disjoint parts of a partition: a time range in the request (#910, section "Time selection" below), or an admitted pool zarrs runs inside.
    3. Retention plateaus (R4). Thirty read passes of every partition in one process: peak RSS 138.87 MB after the first and 138.95 MB after the thirtieth with one worker, 518.85 and 519.34 MB with four. zarrs_filesystem 0.3.14 keeps one lock per key it has touched, never removed: bounded by the store's keys (6,977 files here), so it grows with the distinct keys a process visits, not with passes. Before finding 2's change the one-worker peak crept from 163.2 to 165.4 MB over 20 passes, which the busy rayon threads explain. zarrs_filesystem is now pinned (=0.3.14) beside zarrs.
    4. Synchronizing each replaced chunk costs about a fifth of an update pass. An MSv4 update replaces each chunk through a temporary file synchronized before its rename, and Rust's sync_all on macOS is F_FULLFSYNC. Replacing 576 files of 2.9 MB (the pass's chunk count and mean size) on the same disk took 6.9–7.9 s with a full synchronization each and 4.0 s with one at the end, so about 3–4 s of the 17.6 s pass. Its 64 finishes, which write each partition's last chunk-row and synchronize the directories, took 3.2 s. That is the price of each chunk being old or new after a power loss; workers updating disjoint partitions would overlap it.
    5. Omitting padding needs occupancy up front. The creation plan fixes the row count, and padding is known only from the weights, so with padding omitted to_msv2 reads every weight twice: about 0.9 s of CPU on pilot.ms (7.8 s against 7.0 s), lost in the disk's pace here. D23's default, every cell a row, needs no such pass. A presence role in the batch (contracts.md section 8, "Occupancy") would reduce the pass to a read of the mask alone.
    6. SIGMA into an MSv2 is a policy, not a mapping. The architecture's loss table asks that SIGMA not be rebuilt as 1/√WEIGHT without a justified relation. But SIGMA is a required MAIN column (casacore's MeasurementSet::requiredTableDesc), so every row needs a value, and an MSv4 holds no sigma: refusing would make every MSv4 unconvertible, and a sentinel such as −1 would make tools that weight by 1/σ² (CASA's, and production gaincal) treat every sample as unusable. The converter applies CASA's own rule for corrected-to-data and makes the caller accept it. The owner kept this as the interim rule (2026-10-26), to be revisited with step 4's SIGMA weight role. Revisited: with the role in place the rule is consistent: 1/σ² of the derived SIGMA is the MSv4's weight to rounding (checked in an_msv4_converts_to_an_msv2_of_every_acquisition), and a weight that is not positive becomes σ = −1 and so weight 0, so a solve weighted as CASA's weighs the converted MSv2 as it weighs the MSv4 under SigmaFallback::StoredWeights. What stays a loss is information the MSv4 never held: a sigma that differed from 1/√weight.
    7. Fast creation and per-element flags. Production's fast creation stream (one shape, background writer) takes a row flag per row and no per-element FLAG, CORRECTED_DATA or MODEL_DATA, so to_msv2 uses the variable-shape writers, which the simulator calls several times slower. Here the disk, not those writers, sets the pace (8 s of CPU in 16 s).
    8. A round trip renames. XRADIO's names are suffixes on the MSv2's (field NAME_FIELDID, antenna NAME_STATION, window NAME_SPWID, source NAME_SOURCEID), and to_msv2 writes the MSv4's names, so MSv2 → MSv4 → MSv2 renames every field, window and source (and a VLA's antennas), and repeating it suffixes again. Undoing a suffix would guess at what XRADIO added (a native MSv4 field may be named x_0), so the names stay the MSv4's. Owner decision (2026-10-27): keep the MSv4's names for now; full round trips should be rare outside testing, and stripping the suffixes can be reconsidered if it proves necessary.

    Not done: an MSv2 WEIGHT_SPECTRUM or FLAG_CATEGORY written; feeds other than 0; the MSv4 processor_info (written empty, where XRADIO writes the PROCESSOR row's type and sub-type, so a lone correlator's sub-type is not carried; to_msv2 writes one correlator); fields that follow an ephemeris or whose direction is a polynomial in time, refused in either direction since the MSv2 adapter's catalogue would hold an offset or a constant term as the phase centre; MSv2 subtable rows beyond the catalogue (an MSv2 → MSv2 conversion drops SOURCE detail, HISTORY and POINTING's NAME, TARGET and TRACKING, as reported); a loss inventory at subtable-column level; a v4 → v2 reference converter to compare with (XRADIO has none; casacore read-back stands in); MSv2 creation from parallel workers.

    Time selection: workers split a partition by time¶

    Step (4)'s first item (#910): a request can select times, so workers can share one partition without a hidden pool (converter finding 2). AccessRequest::times is every grid time or those within one half-open range of epochs, stated in the partitions' own time origin and scale (contracts, section 4), so selecting a time rounds nothing; TimeRange::of_grid states a slice a planner cut at grid indices. The estimate gives the selection's times and its first (first_time); the MSv2 adapter reads the selected rows from its opening index, the MSv4 adapter the chunks the selection touches; the MSv2 updater plans and orders its writes over the selection, and the MSv4 one already addressed times by value.

    Groups keep the keys they have over the whole grid. A traversal delivers and closes the part of each group its selection holds and marks a group the selection cuts (GroupFragment::clipped). Workers that split a request plan the slices together: TimeSplit::plan takes consecutive cuts, checks that the slices tile the request in every partition, and counts from the store's group keys (Observation::group_keys) the groups more than one slice holds. The slices share one completion (SplitCompletion, a SliceCompletion handle per slice), which makes a divided group ready once, after every slice has closed its part and all the work begun on it has finished, whatever order the slices run in. #910 first proposed a shared completion that counted closings only, so slice 2's closing fragment could make the group ready before slice 1's work on it had begun; the owner chose the shared completion as the cleaner interface for application programmers, and it counts parts as well as work. clipped stays for a traversal of one range on its own.

    Field selection: imaging's selections on the request¶

    Step 2 of the imaging port (#932, docs/measurement-set/imaging-port.md §2.3, option (a)): the imager selects fields, UV distances and intents per MAIN row today. On batches, field and intent are per time, and UV distance is per sample. - AccessRequest::fields reads every field, or the whole times phased to some fields (FieldSelection::Only). With the time range it resolves at binding to runs of consecutive grid times (TimeRuns, the estimate's time_runs). - A batch holds whole times of one run, so its times stay consecutive on the grid and an updater addresses them as before. - The MSv2 adapter reads only the selected rows. The MSv4 adapter reads the chunks each batch touches, so a chunk row holding several runs is decoded once per batch, with its gap times (contracts, section 4; #935). - Intents need no request field: the adapters partition by them (as XRADIO's converter does), so a partition's samples share their intents and a consumer selects intents by choosing partitions. Two differences from CASA: - CASA matches an intent pattern against the whole OBS_MODE string; the catalogue keeps a partition's intents split, sorted and deduplicated, so a pattern tied to an intent's place in the string can select otherwise when matched intent by intent. - Selection by STATE_ID has no counterpart: the states of one OBS_MODE share a partition. - UV distance stays with imaging, tested per sample as it packs (step 4).

    Group keys are taken over the selected fields' times, as production gaincal bins only the rows it selects. A group keyed by field is held whole or not at all. A group combining fields holds the selected fields' times alone, with bins counted from the earliest of them (production gaincal counts from the lowest-numbered field's first time: #936), so a field selection cuts no group and marks nothing clipped (a time range still does). Time splits tile the selected runs, and updaters claim and write them. An update session merges each updater's runs per variable and checks claims in time order, so a selection of many short runs claims cheaply.

    Geometry inputs: what imaging geometry reads beside the batches¶

    Step 3a of the imaging port (#932, docs/measurement-set/imaging-port.md §2.2.1): today's per-row imaging geometry reads inputs the boundary did not carry. Now both adapters carry them and the converter carries them both ways: - Field reference epochs and per-row frames. A field read from an MSv2 carries FIELD.TIME as its reference epoch (FieldEntry::reference_epoch). PHASE_DIR with a variable reference column (PhaseDir_Ref, as ALMA writes it) resolves per row; fields in different frames are refused, since a data group has one UVW frame and MSv4 one phase-centre frame. An MSv4 field has no reference epoch. - Pointing, as a query, not catalogue content: Observation::pointing returns a partition's pointing series over a span of times within a byte budget, each antenna's samples within it and its nearest either side, and PointingSeries::bracket evaluates the samples around one (antenna, time) there (GLOSSARY, "Pointing Bracket"). MSv2 reads POINTING through today's imaging reader (PreparedSelectedPointingCatalog), so its brackets are that reader's, bit for bit but for one tie (below). That reader's covering rule is casa-rs's, not casacore's pointingIndex (#948). MSv4 reads POINTING_BEAM[time_pointing, antenna_name, local_sky_dir_label] (XRADIO's layout, time accepted for time_pointing, an n_polynomial of one accepted and more refused, since XRADIO defines no origin for them), in fk5, icrs or altaz. Directions stay in the store's frame; converting them is the geometry owner's. - Receptors per partition (PartitionEntry::receptors), no longer per antenna: an MSv2's FEED rows as casacore's MSIter and XRADIO's converter select them (the window's own rows once any row names a window, else the rows for every window), an MSv4 dataset's antenna_xds. Of rows of one antenna, feed and window that differ, the last in table order is read, as casacore's MSIter reads it, and a conversion reports them as a FEED column loss (owner decision on #946, 2026-10-28). An MSv2 destination writes one FEED row per antenna where every antenna states the same receptors in every window it appears in, and otherwise a row per window it states receptors in, so that casacore reads each back; a window stating none (ALMA's WVR window) then has no row, as ALMA writes FEED, and rows of no receptors are written only when no antenna states any. An MSv4 destination omits the receptor variables of a dataset whose source has no FEED row for it, as XRADIO's converter does for ALMA's WVR window, although XRADIO's schema marks polarization_type and receptor_label required (the simulator writes window 0's rows only: #943).

    What a conversion carries of the pointing is planned before anything is written (convert/pointing_plan.rs): - Into an MSv4, each partition's rows within its times (as XRADIO takes rows within each dataset's times) and each antenna's nearest either side, each as its direction at its own time. Pointing in several frames or a frame MSv4 does not name is lost whole, as a reported loss, rather than refused part way through writing. - Into an MSv2, every row of the partitions' antennas, in (time, antenna) order: an MSv2 source's antennas each read once, grouped so that each read fits the budget, an MSv4 source's datasets whole (a sample two datasets hold written once). An MSv4's samples become rows of INTERVAL 0 with TARGET repeating the direction. Rows of antennas no partition names are a counted loss. - Each read is checked against the batch budget with the catalog's own bound (catalog_peak_bytes, the sum of every check the reader makes), and an MSv2 destination's gathered rows with the writer's; beyond it the conversion is refused before anything is written. Rows a read would refuse (more than four polynomial terms, times that are not finite) lose the pointing whole. - The MSv4 reader finds the antennas' nearest samples beyond the requested times together, reading every antenna's directions outward in blocks of 4,096 times that double up to 16,384, so each chunk is decoded once.

    Weights from sigmas: the solve weight role¶

    Step (4)'s second item (example B's finding): a solve weights by 1/σ² of the stored sigmas, as CASA's do, so the request names what the weight role holds (contracts, section 4). WeightRequest::Stored reads the stored weights as before; WeightRequest::InverseSigmaSquared reads SIGMA_SPECTRUM from an MSv2 whose MAIN has both it and WEIGHT_SPECTRUM, else SIGMA broadcast over channels (VisBufferImpl2's rule), and makes each value a weight by casa_observation::inverse_sigma_squared, 0 where σ is not positive (resetWeightsUsingSigma), before padding is spread. An MSv4 has no sigmas, so the request names a fallback: SigmaFallback::Refuse refuses a traversal that reads weights, SigmaFallback::StoredWeights reads the stored weights. The estimate records each partition's rule and column (PartitionEstimate::weights), so a fallback taken is visible to the planner, and an updater refuses to write weights beside a traversal of sigma-derived ones. Production gaincal keeps its own statement of the rule until it moves onto batches (casa-calibration depends on casa-observation only in its tests), and the parity law below holds the two equal.

    Keep, change or discard: the trial's summary for design review¶

    Revision 1 kept what the review asked to keep and made the changes it asked for (table at the top); revision 2 implemented the walkthrough's decisions (second table). What remains open:

    Keep¶

    • One boundary, one interchange structure. Observation (catalogue plus bind_read) is the only thing the two consumers see; behind it the same VisibilityBatch fed the production imaging pass and the production gain accumulator from MSv2 and MSv4 with bit-identical science. Dispatch is one virtual call per bind and per batch.
    • ndarray views under thin types. Channel windows, time windows, transposes for the calibration layout, the [T·B, F, P] view and the zero-stride weight view all borrow without copying; the MSv4 view is the decode buffer itself.
    • Shared quantities in their stored form. Shared::{PerSample, PerTime, Constant} carries an MSv4 interval once per batch and a scan once per time without expanding them, and batch.interval_s(sample) resolves any form, so consumers need no format rule.
    • One checked construction path. Every batch, including packed windows, is built through BatchFill::finish, the one place lengths, identities and fragments are checked.
    • Input completion separate from work completion, joined by GroupCompletion in the consumer.
    • Native Measures views whose references are resolved, or refused, at opening.

    Open questions for the next iteration¶

    Each of these was decided in revision-2-design.md (D12, D17, D16, D5, D25, D18 and D23 in order) and implemented in revision 2, with the qualifications recorded there; they are kept here as the record of what revision 1 left open. What revision 2 leaves open is in that record's last section: the time tolerance of the MSv2 grid, ephemeris as a content key, MSv2 tile-level amplification, strided subset decodes, and an MSv2 creator from batches.

    1. End of stream without a buffer. next_owned returns the buffer at the end, as the review asked, but still needs one to ask; with an exhausted pool that costs a wait (measured above). next_owned(Option<…>) would answer End without one.
    2. Correlations and flag policy in the request. Production imaging routes every stored correlation, so a flagged cross hand drops the Stokes I sample, as CASA does; gaincal flags a channel if any correlation is flagged. The request should name the correlations a consumer reads and the flag policy over unread ones.
    3. Validity cost. Validity::excluded is evaluated per element, with up to four optional checks; revision 4's examples evaluate it once per batch or fragment with mask_into. Folding the rule into the fill remains an option.
    4. Solve weights (example B finding after merging main): production gaincal weights by 1/σ² as CASA does, and the batch had no SIGMA; see example B. (FLAG_ROW in production gaincal, the earlier finding here, is fixed on main.) Resolved in step 4: the request reads 1/σ² weights.
    5. Cross-store rebinding as a contract. The calibration comparison rebinds by context in test code; the design's CalibrationBinding is still to be built and tested with a caltable. Resolved in step 4 (section "Caltable round trip").
    6. One vocabulary. Move CorrelationType from the imaging model into casa-observation (or casa-types), and add a UVW measure to casa-types; UvwView still carries a DirectionRef.
    7. Creation catalogue for MSv2. The writer's declaration is the common Catalogue plus a TimeBaselineGrid; an MSv2 creator needs a rows description in its place. Answered by D23 and the converter: the catalogue and batches suffice, every cell a row (converter section).

    Discard or defer¶

    • The runtime's NativeBlock copy: the trial kept it so the production pass ran unchanged. M2 should let the pass place directly from batch views (NativeRow already borrows slices).
    • The MSv2 opening index reads every row's key columns, in chunks of 2¹⁸ rows, and keeps four bytes per grid cell (scale check above). Its time, about 0.2 µs a row, is what M2 should watch on the largest MeasurementSets; lazy construction per partition remains the option.
    • Pool buffers are bounded by their envelope (revision 4), but allocations inside zarrs or casacore are not counted: the envelope plus each cursor's counted scratch is not yet an admission bound for the whole read.

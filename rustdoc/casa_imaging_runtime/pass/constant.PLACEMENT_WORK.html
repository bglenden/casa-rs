The gridding work of one placement besides its taps, in taps, which region strips are balanced on: each placement adds its kernel’s taps and this to its anchor row’s work (Router::owner_and_work).

A placement costs more than its taps in proportion. Most of the excess is in the tap loops themselves: each spread and gather call sets up the sample’s pointing ramp, and the short rows of a small support cost about twice as much per tap as long ones. The routing and residual work outside the loops is about 2% of a B3 residual pass (#449). Kernels of every size meet in a pass: AW and W-projection supports grow with |w|, so short baselines near the uv centre bring many small kernels and long ones a few large ones. Strips balanced on taps alone then give the centre’s owner several times the placements, and it takes longest.

The value is fitted to the region owners of B3’s residual passes on the M4’s CPU backend in f64 (#449): busy time a · taps + b · placements, with a about 7.6 ns per tap and b about 1.3 µs per placement, from owners holding equal taps and 1.07, 3.63 and 1.73 million placements. The fit predicts the third owner to 1%. With every AW cell kept resident it still balances them to 5%. It is a property of the tap loops, not of the problem: a cheaper small-support path would lower it, so refit it when they change. Metal region passes use it too, unmeasured; any positive value is nearer their cost than none wherever kernel sizes vary. A constant with one kernel size throughout (the spheroidal or a mosaic’s) scales every row’s work alike and moves no strip boundary, since Partition::regions_balanced compares the work exactly.

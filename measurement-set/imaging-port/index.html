    Imaging on the shared observation batches (M2 packet c)¶

    Status: accepted by the owner on 2026-10-27 (section 3); steps 1–4 landed, step 5 in progress (#932) Truth class: accepted design Date: 2026-10-27 Repository baseline: 84824df Design inputs: implementation plan §4 (M2) and D4, contracts, trial README example A

    The plan's M2 packet (c) moves imaging's source preparation and operator windows onto the shared batches, within the existing single pass/worker/resource architecture. This document has three parts: - how imaging reads a MeasurementSet today (section 1); - the design that replaces it, and the owner's decisions on it (sections 2 and 3); - the order of work, and the baseline and blocking comparison that gate the cutover (sections 4 and 5).

    1. Today¶

    Imaging reaches a MeasurementSet only through casa-ms's selected-observation layer. That layer also computes imaging's geometry, which is why casa-ms depends on casa-imaging-model.

    • Compile (casa-imaging-application/src/compile/mod.rs:94) walks MAIN three times:
    • the selection survey, with the MFS spectral envelope (compile/selection.rs:157);
    • the cube density padding (selected_observation/spectral_evaluation.rs:427);
    • the pointing query domain (casa-ms/src/observation_owner.rs:374).

    It then resolves a SelectedObservationResolutionRequest into an ObservationSnapshotInput and its access. - Admission (casa-imaging-runtime/src/source_access.rs:131) plans a content budget that binary-searches rows_per_block (selected_observation/content_plan/requirements.rs:87). - Delivery goes through MeasurementSetSource, the only production BoundedSource (casa-imaging-application/src/imaging/source.rs:131, trait at casa-imaging-runtime/src/pass/mod.rs:175). One producer thread with two block slots runs, per block: 1. consecutive selected MAIN rows of one DATA_DESC_ID (selected_observation/access.rs:522); 2. the channel window with its frame conversion (access/channel_window.rs:14); 3. typed channel-range fills into a buffer (selected_observation_buffer.rs:479); 4. row geometry: phase and pointing laws, per-domain UVW rotation and phase shift, parallactic angle, antenna class, output-frame frequency conversion cached per (field, time) (access/geometry.rs:41, access.rs:887); 5. a stride gather into NativeBlock [row][channel][correlation] (imaging/source.rs:296).

    That is two copies: store to buffer, then buffer to block. - Consumers place samples (pass/wave.rs:238). The imaging weight of every sample is cached across passes in WeightCache, positionally per (selected row, native channel) (pass/weights.rs:17). - savemodel writes through SelectedVisibilityWrite, addressed by RowAddress { physical_row, data_description }, one cell at a time in batches of 10,000 rows (observation_owner/visibility_write.rs:253).

    MAIN-row concepts run through every layer: - SelectedRows, SelectedObservationRunRow and SelectedNumericRow (model); - the survey's row manifest and first_cross_row (compile); - one-DDID blocks and per-row predicates (casa-ms); - RowAddress, BlockShape.rows, and WeightCache's row order (runtime); - non-finite errors that name a MAIN row (application).

    Example A of the trial (tests/ms_trial_imaging.rs) already drives the production operator and major cycle from batches through a BoundedSource over &dyn Observation, on both stores. It skipped all of step 4: stored UVW, native frequencies, one partition, one domain, placeholder row headers and no write-back.

    2. Target¶

    Imaging reads &dyn Observation batches only. Its geometry compiles from the catalogue and the batch. casa-ms keeps the storage adapters and nothing imaging-specific, as D4 proposes. The same imaging run then works on MSv2 and on MSv4.

    2.1 The source¶

    ObservationSource replaces MeasurementSetSource as the production BoundedSource, in casa-imaging-application:

    • begin binds one traversal per wave. Its AccessRequest names:
    • the selected partitions and time range;
    • the wave's channel window (restrict): the native channels whose samples reach the wave's planes, with both interpolation partners;
    • the content (Roles::FLAGS | WEIGHTS for density passes, as FlagsAndWeights asks today).

    A partition replaces a DATA_DESC_ID. - fill packs one batch's valid samples into NativeBlock in one copy, as the trial's pack does. Padding cells (NaN weight) are dropped, and so are the samples a selection predicate excludes (2.3). A block holds whole times of one partition, already in baseline order, so order_rows_by_baseline has nothing to do. - maximum_block comes from the estimate: the widest partition's batch at the admitted batch size.

    The producer thread, two block slots, the worker team, the chunk planner and admission stay as they are. One copy (store to batch buffers) plus the pack replaces the two copies of today.

    As landed in step 4 (#932): ObservationSource (casa-imaging-application/src/imaging/observation_source/) is the batch route, chosen per run by ImagingRequest::source_route, a run option that no imager parameter names. It sees only &dyn Observation. - Selection. Compile still resolves the request against the MeasurementSet and walks MAIN (2.4 is not done). compile::partitions maps that selection to the partitions of the same MeasurementSet's MSv2 adapter: a partition is read when its data description is selected and, under an intent selection, its OBS_MODE is a selected state's. The adapter keys partitions by the whole OBS_MODE string, so a partition holds the rows of every state with its mode (two modes listing the same intents in another order are two partitions), and rows without a state share the empty mode. An intent selection is therefore refused unless the partitions it reads hold the selected states' rows alone: no state that MAIN's rows hold and the selection leaves out has a selected state's mode (intent='0' is refused once rows hold a second state of state 0's mode, and images as today while none does). Fields go in the request. The UV distance is tested per sample, on stored UVW and in wavelengths of the window's REF_FREQUENCY, by the rule today's route now shares (UvSelection::holds). This module is the batch route's only store-specific code; it goes when compile resolves the selection against the catalogue. - Opening. The MSv2 adapter refuses at opening what its grid cannot hold: two rows of one time and baseline in a partition, two fields or two scans at one time of a partition, and a partition's rows with several PROCESSOR_IDs (XRADIO's rule). Today's route images such MeasurementSets; the batch route does not, until the adapter does. Opening asks a grant for what its index will hold before allocating it (Msv2Observation::open_granted): its chunk of key columns, each partition's distinct times, baselines and processors as the first pass finds them (at most one row's keys beyond what was granted), then the scans and the grids. The batch route admits each increase into the opening's reservation, so an index beyond what the policy leaves free is refused, as an admission, before it is allocated, also when runs share a process. Times that differ by baseline (baseline-dependent averaging, jittered TIME) make a cell per time and baseline, which this refuses before allocating. - Delivery. Each wave binds one request per partition in turn, and a batch holds whole times. Padding (every flag set and every weight not finite) and the samples the UV selection excludes are dropped. Samples the store flags, FLAG_ROW included, are kept and given geometry, and a sample whose every routed cell is flagged carries the row flag. A non-finite input fails as today, naming the spectral window, time, baseline, channel and correlation. One divergence: under FiniteValuePolicy::RejectAll today's route fails on a stored row whose every flag is set and no weight is finite, which the batch route reads as padding and drops. - Pointing. When geometry reads pointing, each partition's samples are queried over the span of its selected times (time centroids included when geometry reads them). An antenna's pointing is a property of the antenna and the time, so partitions asking for the same antennas over the same span in the same time system (origin and scale) share an answer (the contract of Observation::pointing). The source keeps the answers across partitions and passes, so a store whose query reads a whole table (the MSv2 adapter scans POINTING) reads it once a distinct query. The charge is the most any query of the observation can hold (Msv2Observation::pointing_bytes: every POINTING row as a candidate, a scan block of at most every row, and the series), the bound today's route charges. A new query may hold what the answers kept leave of it; when the store refuses that (OverBudget), the answers go and it is asked again with all of it, under which no query is refused for its size and POINTING is read in one block of up to 65,536 rows. - Restricted waves. A partition's channel window is bound before its batches are read, from the range of output-frame conversion factors its selected (field, time)s span. It holds every channel today's per-block windows hold; the extra channels reach no plane of the wave. Both the factors and the pointing spans come from one payload-free survey: at the first traversal when geometry reads pointing, and with the factors at the first restricted wave. The survey reads in buffers of its own, one channel and no payload a batch. Under linear interpolation the native channel width is taken from a row's first two delivered channels, so a gapped selection's fine grid can depend on the window delivered, on either route (#964). - Batches and admission. Each partition's times per batch are sized as today's rows per block: at least one time of each partition and at most all its selected times (as today's blocks hold at most the selected rows), and growth up to the bootstrap budget or the source's share of a quarter of free memory. The charge holds the largest batch with its decode scratch, its geometry over the widest native span a batch delivers, the parallactic cache at every selected (time, field), the pointing charge when geometry reads pointing, the survey's batch and conversions, and the passes' blocks. Opening is admitted before the MeasurementSet opens, from MAIN's row count (Msv2Observation::opening_bytes: a cell and eight bytes of time tables a row, and a chunk of key columns), and raised by what it asks for beyond; once open the charge becomes the index (Msv2Observation::index_bytes). Counting laws: - the whole run's (the_observation_batch_route_stays_within_its_admission, with a 101-channel window imaged whole and gapped, 0:0~1;100), whose slack hides the source's charge; - the source's own (tests/batch_source_memory.rs, through source_law), which reads the source alone on an opened, admitted route and fails when the batches' charge is dropped; - each smaller term's owner's: geometry and the parallactic cache (batch_geometry_memory), a traversal's batch envelope (the adapters' laws), and the opening and pointing bounds (casa-ms msv2_opening_memory, which also refuses a jittered-time opening before its grids); the pointing answers kept by unit tests of the source's cache.

    Neither route charges the tiles the table layer stages while a tiled column is read (up to 8 a column, #965); the source's law allows exactly those. - Refused until a later step: a field with EPHEMERIS_ID (#942); an ephemeris-table phase centre (batch geometry follows a body Measures names, and a table directory named like a body would be followed as the body); FLOAT_DATA; several MeasurementSets; and a WEIGHT_SPECTRUM that only some selected rows define (the MSv2 adapter takes the column from its first cell, today's route from every selected row). - Evidence. The route law (casa_imaging_application::route_law) images every T1 run on both routes and compares every product: all 200 are bit-identical, the image runs with usepointing (AW over two windows, the mosaic gridder) among them. On T1.5 (the refim and kernels harnesses, the AW pair with usepointing, and CAS-7841 under the mosaic gridder with usepointing), 53 runs and 442 products agree to a normalized RMS of 1.3e-10 at most, again after each review's fixes. Receipts: NAS casa-rs/issue-932/step4/, step4/review-fixes/, step4/review-fixes-2/ and step4/review-fixes-3/ (the last at the head).

    2.2 Geometry¶

    Row geometry moves from casa-ms into imaging (casa-imaging-model, compiled once, applied per batch), keyed by what a batch carries:

    Today, per MAIN row On a batch
    UVW rotation and phase shift per domain or facet per (time, baseline) from the batch's stored UVW, rotated per (partition, field, time)
    Parallactic angle per (antenna, time), from the catalogue's antenna positions and the batch's times
    Output-frame frequency conversion, cached per (field, time) per (partition, field, time), the same cache key without the DDID
    AW pointing pixel per (DDID, field, time) per (partition, field, time)
    Antenna class (mosaic or heterogeneous primary beams) per antenna, from the catalogue

    Measures come from the authoritative types in casa-types and the bulk conversion API that M1 trialled. The MS-specific helpers (MsCalEngine's frame paths, spectral_selection.rs's prepared frequency factors) fold into Measures as the plan requires, with no translation layer left behind.

    Law: for every T1 and T1.5 workload, the batch geometry must equal today's per-row geometry to 1e-12 relative, sample for sample. Only summation order may differ.

    As landed in step 3b (#932): casa_imaging_model::GeometryPlan compiles once from the problem and the catalogue and evaluates each batch; the shared arithmetic (casacore's UVWMachine, the prepared frequency conversion and the position angle in casa-types; GridFtProjection, the pointing law and the AW pointing groups in casa-imaging-model) moved out of casa-ms, whose route now calls it. casa_imaging_application::geometry_law runs both routes on one MeasurementSet for one request and compares every row, matched by MAIN row number; a row matched twice, a row not matched and a NaN on either side fail it. The T1 fixture checks it after every T1 run and two T1 laws add usepointing (AW over two windows, the mosaic gridder). On all 27 T1 runs (1.05 M rows) and 55 T1.5 runs on the Mac mini (5.59 M rows: 18 refim and 34 kernels cases; the two mawproject cases with usepointing; CAS-7841; the four native-AW variants, which need the EVLA surface from ~/.casa/data, did not run), every quantity of the two routes on the same MeasurementSet is bit-identical, inside the law's 1e-12 bound: projections, pointing, parallactic angles, AW pointing pixels, aperture classes and output-frame frequencies. An MSv4 conversion of the same data is not bit-identical; 2.2.1 records what it changes. Receipts: NAS casa-rs/issue-932/step3b/.

    GeometryPlan::apply computes geometry for the samples its row mask names. Today's route computes it for every selected row, FLAG_ROW rows included, so step 4's mask keeps flagged rows and drops only padding (a NaN weight on both stores) and the samples the selection excludes.

    A moving phase centre follows a body or source Measures names. The two others today's route takes, a field's own ephemeris (TRACKFIELD) and an external ephemeris table, are refused when the plan compiles. A batch whose frequencies convert into or out of the rest frame is refused too: that needs the radial velocity only an ephemeris gives. TRACKFIELD comes with the field ephemerides of #942; the external table is a request input, which GeometryPlan must take before step 4 images through it. The compiled law (PhaseCentreLaw::Ephemeris) names a body and a table alike, a table being a directory of that name, so it must also say which it is by then: a table directory named like a body (Mars) would otherwise be followed as the body.

    2.2.1 What geometry needs from the boundary¶

A map of today's geometry (casa-ms derived/engine.rs, selected_observation/access/geometry.rs, selected_pointing.rs, ephemeris.rs) found six inputs that the boundary does not carry. The converter refuses or drops the same inputs, so section 3's decision 5 (MSv4 T1 at cutover) needs them on both stores.

    The batch already carries the rest: stored UVW and time centroids per (time, baseline), field per time, and antennas per baseline. The catalogue carries the antenna positions, mounts, dish diameters, telescope names, and the partitions' frequencies and frames.

    As landed in step 3a (trial README, "Geometry inputs"): field reference epochs, per-row phase-centre frames, pointing and per-partition receptors. Field ephemerides take a PR of their own, which must merge before the cutover (#942); until then the converter refuses them, as it did.

    Two consequences: - Pointing in MSv4. XRADIO keeps one polynomial term per sample and defines no origin for more, so an MSv2 row with NUM_POLY > 0 converts as its direction at its own time, the further terms a reported loss; the MSv2 adapter keeps today's evaluation. MSv4 states no interval per sample, so a converted store's brackets have no covering sample, and imaging's law chooses between the samples either side (nearest, or interpolation). For pointing sampled at the integration, that can pick a different sample than the MSv2 did. Step 3b measured it (#958), and the owner decided (2026-10-28, #946) that the MSv4 route's choice stands, with no intervals recovered. The converter reports INTERVAL, TARGET, NAME and TRACKING as a table loss. Today's covering rule is casa-rs's, not casacore's (#948), and the batch route keeps it (#958). Measured in step 3b (the batch geometry of an MSv2 against its MSv4 conversion, geometry_law::compare_stores): on ALMA CAS-7841, whose POINTING is sampled every 48 ms, imaged with the mosaic gridder and usepointing, the MSv4 route's pointing differs from the MSv2's by at most 1.4e-10 rad (0.03 mas) and its UVW and phase shift by under 1e-15 relative; on the EVLA mawproject pair under AW projection with usepointing, not at all. The native AW workloads need the EVLA surface from ~/.casa/data, which the Mac mini lacks. The owner kept the boundary's tie rule for the cutover (2026-10-28, #946). - AW pointing epochs. Today's epochs follow MAIN order, which interleaves windows; batches are partition-major. Refresh decisions carry state across consecutive epochs, so the order can change which epochs refresh. CASA's imager iterates by field, then window, then time. Step 3b instruments both orders against CASA before choosing, and the equality law runs on the native AW workloads. As landed in step 3b: the batch route carries the refresh state in delivery order, partition by partition and each in time order (GeometryTraversal). On every workload measured (T1's two-window AW with usepointing, the EVLA mawproject pair with usepointing) its pixels equal today's MAIN-order plan bit for bit. That is not so in general: today's plan forms an epoch per (data description, field, time) in MAIN order and counts its rows before channel windows skip any block, where the batch route counts each (partition, time)'s row mask. Where those counts differ (windows at one time holding different numbers of rows, or a window a run's channels skip), refresh decisions can diverge. Both casa-rs orders refresh at every field change; CASA's field-major order does not, so a multi-field AW mosaic with usepointing can group differently from CASA on either route. No acceptance workload images AW with usepointing over several fields (native-aw-evla runs without it); instrumenting CASA's epochs is #957.

    2.3 Selection¶

    The imager's selection is resolved once (RowSelection, casa-imaging-model/src/observation.rs:188): fields, a UV-distance interval (in wavelengths per window) and scan intents. A compiled predicate applies it per MAIN row (selected_observation/row_selection.rs:20). Windows, channels, correlations and times already map onto AccessRequest. On a batch: - field and intent are per time; - UV distance is per (time, baseline), scaled by the partition's wavelength.

    There are two options: - (a) AccessRequest states field and intent selections, which the adapters apply to whole times, and imaging tests the UV distance as it packs; - (b) imaging tests all three as it packs.

    Option (a) skips the decoding of unselected times, which matters for a mosaic imaged one field at a time. It also serves calibration's field and intent selections. It belongs to packet (a), catalogue/selection and read binding.

    As landed in step 2, AccessRequest::fields selects fields, and the adapters deliver the whole selected times in runs (trial README, "Field selection"). Group keys are taken over the selected fields' times, as production gaincal bins only its selected rows (the bin origin with fields combined is #936). On MSv4 a chunk row holding several runs is decoded once per batch (#935). Intents need no request field: the adapters partition by intent, as XRADIO's converter does, so the imager selects intents by the partitions it requests. The catalogue keeps a partition's intents split and sorted, so step 4 matches an intent pattern intent by intent, where CASA matches the whole OBS_MODE string; selection by STATE_ID is not supported (contracts, section 4).

    2.4 Compile without MAIN walks¶

    The opening's index (the MSv2 adapter grids MAIN's key columns once, in row chunks) and the estimate answer what compile walks MAIN for today: - selected sample counts per partition; - the spectral envelope, from the partitions' frequencies and the fields' times; - the cube density padding; - the first cross-correlation time, for native AW; - the pointing domain, from the catalogue's pointing and the selected times.

    Three walks become one index built at opening. The index's cost is the trial's measured opening time; the baseline (section 5) must show the sum does not regress.

    2.5 Weights, WeightCache, errors¶

    • Weight slots. WeightCache keeps one slot per delivered sample, in traversal order. Traversals are deterministic for a given request and batch size, so passes still meet samples in one order. The slot count comes from the estimate's valid samples after selection.
    • Errors. A non-finite value names its (partition, time, baseline, channel, correlation) and the store, not a MAIN row.
    • RowContext keeps field, window (now partition) and original UVW. RowAddress stays the MAIN row of today's route: batch-route rows are unaddressed, and the write-back resolves each block's rows from the provenance its source queues (2.6), since a block is one batch.

    2.6 Write-back¶

    savemodel and the continuum CORRECTED_DATA write through an observation_io update session (step 5). A run that writes opens the MSv2 adapter as every batch run does (the opening grant, §2.1), resolves its selection against it, then hands it to the session (UpdateSession::over_msv2): every pass reads the batches the session's updaters write back, and the MeasurementSet is opened and indexed once.

    • Targets. model (MODEL_DATA) is created when MAIN lacks it, seeded with CASA's initial model, after clearing what CASA's addScrCols clears (#912), as SelectedVisibilityWrite does. corrected (CORRECTED_DATA) is never created: the continuum transform writes an existing column, and today's route also refuses a missing one. An MSv4 store cannot gain a group, so it writes an existing model group or is refused at opening; production reaches MSv4 only after §2.4.
    • Addresses. The passes hand each block to their sink on the consumer thread, after the producer has refilled the batch's buffers, so the sink cannot hold the batch. A delivered batch gives an owned BatchAddress (VisibilityBatch::address): its catalogue, partition, times, channels, products and weight rule, without its payload. An updater writes against an address (Updater::write_at) as it writes against the batch, except for a change whose check reads the payload: an MSv4 store knows its padding only from the batch's weights, so a flag cleared or a weight written by address is refused on every store.
    • Masks. PatchValues::Visibilities carries written, a [sample, channel, product] mask: an element it leaves keeps its stored value, and a sample it leaves wholly is neither read nor written. Imaging leaves padding, samples outside the UV selection, the channels of a batch's span the selection leaves out (a gapped spw), the fit-only channels of a continuum transform and the products not routed: the cells today's route writes, flagged samples included. One difference: the source knows padding by its values (every flag set and every weight not finite, as both adapters pad), so a real row that is wholly flagged with no finite weight is left as padding is. Today's route writes its prediction; the row contributes nothing to any image either way.
    • Destinations. A session opening an MSv2 refuses, before anything is cleared or written, a target column that cannot be written in place in bounded memory, as today's route refuses it: an existing column outside TiledShapeStMan and TiledColumnStMan, or a column to create whose clone of DATA's manager would be one (scratch_columns). A column in another manager is saved by rewriting every row of its storage group, once a batch. The first updater applies the rule again under MAIN's lock, as today's writer checks when it begins, so a column another writer adds meanwhile is refused too.
    • Order. An updater takes its traversal's batches in any order and any of them; the times it is not given keep what they hold. MSv4 updaters already did. The MSv2 updater's delivery-order check and its refusal, at finish, of unwritten times are gone: they guarded a write planned up front, which neither store makes. A batch with no imaged sample yields no block, and nothing is written for it.
    • Provenance. A batch route block is one batch. While the writing traversal runs, the source queues, in delivery order, each partition it binds (its request) and, for each block it fills, the batch's address and the batch sample of each row. The runtime hands a writing pass's sink every block once, in source order (VisibilitySink), so the sink takes the queue's head. It checks the head against the block (one row per recorded sample, each on its sample's baseline) and fails the pass on a mismatch. At most the stream's blocks are queued (casa_imaging_runtime::pass::STREAM_SLOTS).
    • One updater a partition. The batch route binds one request per partition. The sink binds a partition's updater when its request reaches the queue's head, before it finishes the last partition's. Updaters overlap, so the MSv2 session holds MAIN's write lock from the first updater (bound when the first block reaches the sink, which creates and seeds a missing MODEL_DATA) to the end of the pass. SelectedVisibilityWrite takes it, and creates the column, when the writer begins.
    • Reads beside writes. The source's producer reads the next batches while the sink writes the last, on one MeasurementSet in one process, where file locks do not keep them apart. Each save rewrites table.dat, so the MSv2 observation reads what it needs from it (each data group's tile shape) once, when it opens, and an estimate reads no file the saves rewrite. The multi-partition write test found the read that did: about one run in fifteen tore it. Writing table.dat by rename would make a torn read impossible (#983). Each save also clears every cached tile of MAIN, not only the written column's (#984), which step 6's timing weighs. That test also found that the pass runtime dropped a source's failure when its producer stopped first. The stream now reads everything the producer sent (pass_laws a_source_failing_midway_fails_the_pass).
    • Admission. The write stays a Residency::All pass. Its charge (today SelectedVisibilityWrite::retained_bytes) is, on the batch route, the largest of each term over the partitions (BatchRoute::write_bytes), since the sink's buffers keep one partition's capacity while another's updater writes:
    • what its updater holds writing its largest batch (UpdateSession::write_bytes): on MSv2 each present row's cell of each target, as built or merged and as moved into the table, its tiles, and the seeding of a created MODEL_DATA in batches of at most the traversal's batch bytes; on MSv4 one partition's windows and the chunks they replace;
    • the patch the sink assembles (each column's values and the mask over the batch's samples, channels and products);
    • the provenance of the blocks in flight.

    casa-ms's msv2_update_memory holds the updater's bound (created and seeded, masked and merged, two targets). The whole run's law (a_visibility_write_stays_within_its_admission) runs on both routes. The source's law (batch_source_memory) drives writing passes too, one of them over several batches of a partition. - Evidence. The route law images a writing run's batch route on a copy of its MeasurementSet taken before either route writes (route_law::copy_for_batches, <vis>.batches), then compares each column written over every cell, 1,024 rows at a time. The copy needs the MeasurementSet's size again on disk; imager_routes removes it when the law holds.

    The MSv2 counting law found two defects in the trial updater, both fixed in this step. It read the cells it merges through the table's row cache, which loads every row of MAIN (ADR-0008); merges now read past it, in one call per batch. It seeded a created MODEL_DATA from two lists of every MAIN row; seeding now goes a partition's rows at a time. The selected-row mutation also moves its batch's cells into the table rather than cloning them, which saves a copy of each cell on every MSv2 mutation writer.

    2.7 What is removed at cutover¶

    The cutover PR deletes all of the following: - MeasurementSetSource; - the selected-observation access, buffer, content plan and walk modules; - SelectedRows and the row-based model types; - SelectedVisibilityWrite (once nothing else uses it); - the casa-ms → casa-imaging-model edge. Step 3b deepened it: today's route in casa-ms now calls GridFtProjection, the pointing law and the AW pointing groups from the model, and those callers go with the route.

    The imaging dependency checker's grandfathered lists shrink accordingly.

    3. Owner decisions¶

    All five were decided on 2026-10-27, each as proposed below: imaging takes casa-observation and casa-ms drops casa-imaging-model; geometry lives in casa-imaging-model; selection is option (a); the thresholds are as stated; and MSv4 imaging is part of the cutover's acceptance.

    1. Dependency direction (an Ask First item).
    2. Imaging gains casa-observation as a normal dependency: casa-imaging-model (geometry over catalogue types) and casa-imaging-application (the source).
    3. casa-ms loses its normal dependency on casa-imaging-model.
    4. This is D4's proposed amendment. dependency-policy.json needs its exact-set edits.
    5. Where geometry lives. In casa-imaging-model, as D4 says (proposed here), or in a new imaging crate. The proposal keeps one crate.
    6. Selection (2.3). Option (a) puts field and intent selections in AccessRequest (packet a) before this port; option (b) tests everything in the source. The proposal is (a).
    7. Blocking thresholds (section 5). The proposal:
    8. wall time at most +2% or two baseline standard deviations, whichever is larger, per workload and worker count;
    9. peak RSS at most +5%;
    10. images and models equal to today's to a normalized RMS of 1e-6.
    11. MSv4 imaging at cutover. It comes almost free through &dyn Observation. The proposal makes it part of the acceptance (T1 on the trial's MSv4 conversions) rather than a later packet.

    5. Baseline and blocking comparison¶

    Workloads come from resources/imaging-architecture/representative-science-matrix.json: - standard-mfs-dirty-vla (6.28 M samples), standard-mfs-hogbom-vla and standard-mfs-model-data-vla (write-back); - spectral-cube-dirty-vla and spectral-cube-clean-vla; - continuum-subtracted-cube-alma; - mosaic-alma; - w-multifield-cube-vla; - native-aw-evla (pointing); - multidomain-outlier-vla (domains); - T1 (cargo test -p casars-imager --test t1) and the T1.5 refim cases.

    Measured per run: from each <imagename>.summary.json, the per-phase seconds, process_peak_rss_bytes and peak_reserved_bytes; and the admitted block size (today's rows_per_block, then batch times). Each run also records the commit, FFTW link, backend, precision, worker counts and cache state, as TESTING.md "Imaging tiers and evidence" and .agents/skills/casa-rs-imaging-performance require.

    Protocol: - A B B A on the Mac mini, with one worker beside four at every bar. - The baseline is today's route at the commit the cutover branches from; it is re-run if main moves. - The noise band comes from the baseline's own repeats.

    Harness: tools/perf/imager/run_workload.py already runs a workload against CASA (scripts/bench-imager-vs-casa.sh). It gains one mode that runs two casa-rs builds on the same inputs and writes one comparison receipt per workload: medians, the band, verdicts, and images compared by normalized RMS. Receipts go where docs/agent-reference.md puts evidence (small receipts under casa-rs-evidence/imaging-port/, a copy on the NAS), summarized on the PR. Inputs are not copied off the Mac mini.

    Blocking: - A workload outside its thresholds (decision 4) blocks the cutover, whatever correctness says. - A faster route is recorded, not a reason to change thresholds.

    6. Risks¶

    • Opening index cost. Gridding MAIN's keys at opening replaces three walks. On a large MS with a narrow selection it may cost more than today's replay of the selected rows. The trial measured it on pilot.ms only; step 1 measures it on the T2 inputs.
    • Padding. A partition whose times have few baselines (subarrays) pads many cells. Packing drops them, but the batch buffers are sized for the padded grid. The estimate reports the padded bytes, so admission sees them.
    • One producer thread. Today's producer reads in row order. Batches allow parallel cursors (the trial's work units), but the design keeps one producer and leaves parallel production to a measured follow-up.
    • Geometry drift. Moving the measures paths can move UVW or frequencies by rounding. The 1e-12 equality law and T1.5 against CASA bound it. Reproducibility of the last bit is not a goal (AGENTS.md).

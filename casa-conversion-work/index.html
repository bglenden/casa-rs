    Completing CASA conversions in chunks¶

    Truth class: normative Last reality check: 2026-11-12 Verification: just docs-check

    Choose a functional outcome¶

    Divide the authorized scope into ordered chunks of observable CASA behavior. A chunk follows an operation or a connected upstream execution path, such as applying calibration tables, predicting models, or generating pointings. Parameters and test cases describe its coverage. A single parameter value, random seed, interpolation boundary or test failure is usually part of a chunk, rather than another delivery unit.

    Use the accepted brief, the pinned CASA source, existing behavior inventories and CASA's tests to define the list. Include known missing capabilities and known missing evidence. Distinguish them: implemented behavior awaiting a comparison is different from a missing implementation. Do not infer completed behavior from a signature or a method's overall status.

    The work issue is the single place for the ordered list and current status. Keep numerical evidence and detailed API coverage in their existing catalogs and receipts; link them instead of maintaining another competing inventory. For resumed work, map existing changes and valid evidence onto the chunks. Do not restart comparisons merely to populate a new tracking format.

    Record each chunk before implementing it¶

    Each chunk has these fields, compactly in a table or checklist on the issue:

    Field Required content
    Identity Stable ID, observable outcome and order.
    CASA anchor Release and upstream functions or CASA test groups defining the behavior.
    Coverage Methods, parameters, effective defaults, modes and relevant interactions included. Explicitly name boundaries to other chunks or deferred work.
    Acceptance Named independent cases or a bounded case matrix, compared products, tolerances and expected refusals. Add applicable interoperability, backend, performance and memory requirements.
    Starting state What is implemented, which valid evidence already exists, and the actual gaps.
    Dependencies A named scenario and the exact missing capability that blocks it. A whole supporting tool is not a dependency.
    State Queued, implementing, checking, delivered or blocked; current branch/PR and evidence links. A blocker names the action that removes it.

    Choose cases that distinguish the required behavior and cover its meaningful interactions. Do not construct the Cartesian product of every parameter unless the contract requires it. Unknown coverage is recorded as unknown, not silently passed or automatically expanded into a new capability.

    Follow the list¶

    Keep one chunk under implementation. Finish its named cases and related fixes together, normally in one PR for that functional outcome. Do not create a PR for each argument form or boundary case and leave the same outcome unfinished.

    When a finding is outside the active chunk, record it against an existing queued chunk, or add a proposed chunk with the failing scenario and reason it belongs to the authorized scope. Continue the active work. Fix a dependency when it prevents the chunk's acceptance; record why it is necessary. A general cleanup, full supporting-tool conversion or new task does not become authorized because the agent encountered it.

    Change a chunk's coverage explicitly on the issue, stating the trigger and effect on completion. The owner's scope and priority prevail. Do not silently drop requirements to finish, or widen acceptance after each passing run.

    Check a representative case cheaply before a large run. Use focused regressions while editing; run the applicable full gates on the stable chunk. Reuse current green evidence as TESTING.md allows. One required review means one review of the stable diff, not a new review for each parameter variant. None of this waives a gate or changes its tolerance.

    While a chunk waits for CI or review, keep responsibility for it and implement the next independent listed chunk on a separate branch when needed. Do not invent work to avoid waiting. Follow AGENTS.md's continuation and monitoring rules when all remaining chunks are blocked or waiting.

    Complete and report by behavior¶

    A chunk is delivered when its specified behavior and acceptance cases pass, required gates and review are resolved, its evidence and documentation match, and its change has merged. An exception needs the decision required by AGENTS.md. An open limitation stays visible; a useful partial implementation does not become a completed chunk.

    Progress reports name the active chunk, completed requirements, remaining requirements, blocker if any, and next listed chunk. Report progress by accepted behavior, rather than counting PRs, commits or successful test invocations. The authorized task ends at its stated outcome, after all required chunks; finishing a chunk or setting auto-merge does not end it.

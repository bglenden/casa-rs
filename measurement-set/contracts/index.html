    Intermediate observation contracts¶

    Status: revision 7; the read contract the M0/M1 trial realizes, frozen narrowly for M2 by the owner on 2026-10-25 Truth class: design proposal Date: 2026-10-25

    This is the interface between persistent observations and numerical consumers. The governing principle is that MSv4 is the future: the interface takes MSv4's native shape, and the MSv2 adapter assembles MSv2 data into it.

    Sections 1 to 6 and the creation part of section 7 are the current contract: what the trial's casa-observation crate and its casa-ms adapters implement, stated directly, with the profile's refusals. The owner froze them, narrowly, for M2 on 2026-10-25 (#898): M2 builds on them, and a change to them follows the implementation plan's change control (its section 10). The freeze is narrower than the M1 gate. The M1 rows the trial did not complete (trial README, "M1 coverage") stay open, and porting the applications is expected to show where the design is not sufficient; such a finding reopens the section concerned through the same change control. Section 8 holds the provisional capabilities: designed but not realized or not proven, each with the acceptance obligation it must meet before it joins the contract. Updates (section 7) are provisional too. How the design got here (decisions D1 to D25 and the trial's revisions) is recorded in the trial's design record and the trial README, not here.

    The bounded intermediate is VisibilityBatch. Chunk and tile mean physical storage; compute window means a kernel's view. The name does not imply reading visibility payloads for a flags-only request.

    1. Contract rules¶

    Rule Required behavior
    C1: source independence No physical row IDs, Zarr keys, storage managers, or MS-version branches in numerical processing loops.
    C2: faithful values Coordinates and references are native casa-rs Measures types/views, directly accepted by its conversion APIs. Canonical coordinate units; explicit frames and precision, native flag and weight semantics, full data-group association, ordered baselines. No silent scientific transform: what the profile cannot represent is refused.
    C3: layout independence Separate sample topology, memory layout, scientific grouping, and storage chunking.
    C4: bounded ownership Every live payload, index, decode buffer, cache, packed copy and queue is admitted; borrowed views cannot outlive/refill their storage. Independent work can retire separately; shared storage stays live until its last use completes.
    C5: invertible updates Sample-preserving reorder/selection retains a checked inverse source map; reduction does not. Shared variables have explicit mutation scope.
    C6: honest guarantees Unsupported profiles, layouts and native features, and non-atomic writes, are explicit typed refusals.
    C7: bulk execution Bulk conversion is a requirement across Measures and quanta, including future measure families. Bind numeric kind, layout and invariant context outside scalar loops; use native array views and family-specific kernels. No required per-value object allocation, virtual dispatch, coordinate reconstruction or repeated invariant setup/provider lookup.

    2. Topology and identity¶

    The catalogue¶

    An opened observation supplies a Catalogue: antennas, fields, scans, observing sessions and partitions, and for each partition everything a batch needs that does not vary by sample (channel frequencies and widths and their frame, correlation products, the baseline table, the time scale and origin, the data groups with their units and UVW frame). A batch links to its partition's entry and copies none of it. IDs are opaque indices scoped to that opening. They are not casacore subtable row numbers, globally unique names, or serialized identities; backends keep their own mapping. Large pointing and time-dependent metadata are not part of the catalogue and are queried in bounded windows; opening does not build a sample-sized metadata snapshot beyond the partition grid of section 2. Pointing is such a query (Observation::pointing, below). A field's ephemeris is not large: one sample per ephemeris step, not per visibility time. So, as the imaging port's accepted plan proposes (imaging-port.md §2.2.1), it is part of the field's entry (FieldEntry::ephemeris, #942), shaped as MSv4's field_and_source_ephemeris holds it, the field's phase centre then its offset from the body; fields that follow one ephemeris share it, and the catalogue counts its bytes (Catalogue::ephemeris_bytes). The MSv2 adapter reads it; the MSv4 store's reading and writing of it are provisional (section 8).

    A partition is one store unit (an MSv4 dataset; the MSv2 rows that one such dataset would hold), defined by its content: spectral window (frequencies, widths and frame), correlation products, intents, observing session, and, in MSv2 only, the feed pair. The MSv2 adapter partitions rows as XRADIO's converter does by default (DATA_DESC_ID × OBS_MODE × OBSERVATION_ID × EPHEMERIS_ID, and the feed pair), so one observation opens as the same partition set from either store. Each partition also records the fields and scans its samples visit, and its antennas' receptors (polarization types and angles): an MSv4 dataset's antenna_xds holds them, and an MSv2's FEED rows as casacore's MSIter and XRADIO's converter select them, the partition's window's own rows once any row names a window, else the rows for every window (-1). Of FEED rows of one antenna, feed and window that differ (receptors that vary in time), the last in table order is read, as casacore's MSIter reads it (with a warning); XRADIO's converter refuses them, and casa-rs's reports them as a loss (owner decision on #946, 2026-10-28).

    A field's phase centre is stored in one frame for the whole catalogue (a data group has one UVW frame, MSv4 one phase-centre frame). The MSv2 adapter resolves PHASE_DIR's frame per row where the column's reference varies (PhaseDir_Ref) and refuses fields in different frames. A field read from an MSv2 carries FIELD.TIME as its reference epoch, at which a direction in a frame that moves against the sky is converted; an MSv4 field has none, its frame being celestial.

    Pointing is read per partition, never held in the catalogue: Observation::pointing takes a partition, antennas, a span of times in the partition's time system, the direction (the beam's, or MSv2's tracking target, which MSv4 does not hold) and a byte budget, and returns a pointing series: per antenna, the samples within the span and its nearest each side, each a polynomial in time about its own origin, in the store's frame. A pointing bracket evaluates them at one time: the sample whose interval covers it (the nearest), the last at or before it and the first at or after it. MSv2 reads its POINTING rows through today's imaging reader, so its brackets are that reader's bit for bit, except that of two rows equally near a time it covers with the earlier where that reader takes the lower row (the same wherever an antenna's rows are in time order). That reader is casa-rs's, not casacore's: MSPointingColumns::pointingIndex covers with the first row in table order, gives a row of INTERVAL 0 half the gap to the antenna's next row and a negative interval every time (#948). The cutover keeps the boundary's tie rule (owner decision on #946, 2026-10-28). MSv4 reads POINTING_BEAM, whose samples state no interval, so nothing covers and a consumer's law chooses between the samples either side; XRADIO keeps one polynomial term and defines no origin for more, so more are refused.

    An observing session carries the telescope, project and observers, and MSv4's archive identifiers (execution_block_UID, session_reference_UID, scheduling_block_UID). An MSv2 holds those only where an importer recorded them; the adapter reads the ExecBlock and SchedulingBlock UIDs of OBSERVATION::SCHEDULE as XRADIO's converter does, and not ASDM_EXECBLOCK.

    Binding two catalogues¶

    Catalogue::bind_to maps one opening's entities onto another's by content, never by list position or display name (converters rename antennas and fields):

    Entity Matched by
    Antenna station and position, within a stated tolerance
    Field phase centre: frame, and angles within the tolerance
    Scan casacore number, or name when a scan has none
    Session telescope, project and observers, and each archive identifier both sessions state
    Partition the spectral and product content (frequencies and widths bit for bit, frame, products in order, intents), the bound session, the bound set of fields, the bound set of scans when both stores name scans, and the feed pair when both stores have one

    A key one store lacks (MSv4 has no feed axis; an MSv2 may hold no archive identifiers) does not distinguish, but the binding is one to one: two entities that one catalogue keeps apart never bind to the same entity of the other. An entity that matches nothing, more than one, or the same counterpart as another is a typed refusal; an ambiguity is never resolved by position. Ephemeris is not yet a partition key.

    Binding a calibration table¶

    A calibration table numbers antennas, fields and spectral windows by the rows of its own ANTENNA, FIELD and SPECTRAL_WINDOW subtables. Catalogue identities are scoped to one opening, so a table never holds them: a store's persisted namespace (casa_ms::observation_io::persisted_namespace) gives the subtables a table solved on it carries and the row of each catalogue entity there, for the store opened with given options (partitioning by field or scan changes the partitions, so the namespace keeps the catalogue it describes and the writer refuses a solve from another opening). An MSv2's namespace is its own subtables, its catalogue's antennas, fields and sessions being their rows and each partition naming its data description's window, so a table written through it has the rows CASA's gaincal copies and CASA applies it to that MeasurementSet by its own association. An MSv4 has no such rows; its namespace is the subtables its MSv2 conversion holds, in catalogue order, so a table written through it applies by CASA's association only to that conversion, and is bound to any other store. The writer translates a solve's catalogue identities to those rows, copies the subtables and records the store's path as MSName; it never writes a catalogue identity.

    Binding a table to a store (CalibrationBinding, owned by calibration) compares the table's rows with the store's namespace rows, in this order:

    1. Stated associations, as CASA's spwmap states them: a partition's window (several may share one row, including the row an unstated window binds to below, as spwmap leaves a window unlisted there on its own row) and an antenna (one to one).
    2. CASA's own association, where the caller states that the table was written through this store's namespace, as CASA assumes of a table applied to the MeasurementSet it was solved on: each row binds to its own index, even where two rows hold the same content, once the rows are checked consistent (as many of each kind, antenna names agreeing as below; a window may hold a solve over part of its channels). Names, row counts and a gain table's collapsed channels do not establish it: two windows of different channels can collapse to one row alike.
    3. Content otherwise: an antenna by station and position, a field by phase centre, a window by frame and channels, or the one channel a gain table collapses them to, of the summed width (in magnitude: CASA's solve holds widths positive) at the channels' arithmetic mean frequency (CASA's gaincal, whose ChannelAverageTVI averages frequencies unweighted before a G or T solve, whatever the widths, and casa-rs's writer since #917). Names never bind, since converters rewrite them, but an antenna whose name disagrees beyond a converter's _STATION suffixes is refused: another antenna on the same pad does not take its gains. An entity the other side lacks binds to nothing, so an antenna subset applies as CASA applies a table missing an antenna (calflag flags its samples); a field the table lacks likewise has no solutions until a gainfield policy is built. A window that a table window bound to no other window overlaps in frequency is refused (a solve over part of its channels), not left unbound, in any frame: a table solved in one frame and applied to the store regridded to another solves none of its windows, so it must be stated rather than flag every sample. The applier must have opened the store as the namespace's opening did, or binding is refused. Two candidates, or two entities claiming one row, are refused: equal grids or positions alone do not prove two rows are one entity. Directions compare within a stated tolerance: the catalogue holds them as direction cosines, which move an angle by a few units in the last place at each store crossing (#918).

    One topology: the time × baseline grid¶

    A batch is whole times of its partition's time × baseline grid, MSv4's own: sample = time_index × baseline_count + baseline_index. Field and scan are per time; timestamps and baseline pairs are held once (O(T + B) entries, not O(T × B)). There is no irregular topology. The MSv2 adapter places rows on the grid when the MeasurementSet is opened, reading MAIN's key columns in bounded row chunks. Two rows claiming one cell are distinct acquisitions the partition keys failed to separate: opening refuses them, naming both rows, rather than discarding or averaging either.

    A cell no acquisition fills is padding. Every store delivers it flagged, with a NaN weight: the MSv2 adapter pads with flag true and values, weights, UVW and time auxiliaries NaN; an MSv4 store holds its own padding (XRADIO's converter pads with NaN values and weights and leaves FLAG false; casa-rs's writer pads the same way with FLAG true), and the MSv4 adapter folds the non-finite stored weight into the delivered flag. A NaN weight is padding's one mark in both stores; the MSv2 adapter padded with weight 0 until the write-back trial found that an MSv4 written from it could not tell padding from a flagged acquisition. The estimate reports the fraction of cells an adapter pads before anything is read; it is 0 for MSv4, whose store holds its grid whole, and that says nothing of the cells the store itself holds as padding.

    Keep three facts separate:

    • Selection: which logical elements the request includes.
    • Acquisition occupancy: the batch carries absence only as padding, which the delivered flags mark. It does not record whether an MSv4 store's padding was once an MSv2 row; recovering original row existence is a conversion policy (section 8).
    • Validity and flags: numerical validity of requested values and native scientific flag state, varying by channel and correlation.
    // The current shape; casa-observation has it.
pub struct VisibilityBatch<'a> {
    pub catalogue: &'a Arc<Catalogue>, // per-partition constants live there once
    pub partition: PartitionId,
    pub time: EpochView<'a>,              // whole times of the partition's grid
    pub baselines: &'a [OrderedBaseline], // the partition's table, from the catalogue
    pub field: ArrayView1<'a, FieldId>,   // per time
    pub scan: Option<ArrayView1<'a, ScanId>>, // per time
    pub timing: SampleTiming<'a>,         // time centroid, interval, exposure
    pub first_channel: usize,
    pub frequency: FrequencyView<'a>,     // a window of the catalogue's
    pub correlations: &'a [Correlation],  // the delivered products, shared, never copied
    pub group: DataGroupView<'a>,         // [sample, channel, correlation] payload, UVW
    pub fragments: &'a [GroupFragment],   // scientific groups (section 4)
}

    Fields along time¶

    For native MSv4, distinguish a dimension from an auxiliary coordinate: VISIBILITY[time, baseline, frequency, polarization] carries field_name[time]. A sequential mosaic may therefore have fields [A, A, B, B, A] along its time axis without a field axis or any averaging across fields. Optional field-based partitioning produces separate datasets instead; the API preserves the sample-to-field association in either case, including selection and slicing. Per-antenna pointing is a separate association. This does not establish a representation for simultaneous beams or phase centres, or for per-baseline field changes.

    Each data group of an MSv4 dataset has its own field_and_source context and UVW frame, and a traversal of a group delivers that group's fields. Fields from different contexts are distinct unless name, phase centre and source all agree. Before writing, the writer validates that every sample of a time agrees on the field, against the declared grid, and refuses otherwise; it does not copy XRADIO's maximum-ID reduction. Distinct acquisitions never overwrite one another in one time/baseline cell. The converter evidence distinguishes observed upstream behavior from this preservation contract.

    3. Numeric arrays, metadata, and data groups¶

    Units and measure references¶

    The processing interface guarantees canonical coordinate units, so a kernel never inspects a string to discover whether a frequency is in Hz:

    Quantity Processing representation
    Frequency and channel width Hz
    UVW and Cartesian positions/distances m
    Angular coordinates rad, with coordinate representation and reference retained
    Durations, integration intervals and exposure s
    Epochs A split-precision epoch view: time scale, an MjdHighPrec origin and f64 offsets in seconds; never an unlabelled f64 Unix timestamp

    The adapters check every stored unit and reference once, at opening, and refuse what the profile does not cover rather than rescale or reinterpret it. MSv2 TIME and TIME_CENTROID take their fixed MEASINFO reference (UTC by default, per the MeasurementSet definition); a variable reference column, or two columns that disagree, is refused. MSv4 times are read only as format: unix with scale: utc, following astropy, whose Unix epoch is UTC in every scale; any other scale is refused. Requesting other units or a conversion at binding is provisional (section 8).

    Each measure view associates values with quantity kind, unit, reference/frame and representation. References shared by an axis or batch are stored once. There is no per-visibility measure object. A data group's UVW view carries its effective frame: for MSv2, CASA's convention that UVW is computed in the phase centre's frame whatever its MEASINFO says (simulators and fillers commonly label it ITRF), resolved once at opening; MSv4 states it, and a group whose UVW frame is not its phase centres' frame is refused. The stored label stays in the catalogue for faithful writing.

    Unit normalization never performs a reference-frame or time-scale conversion: Hz in TOPO and Hz in LSRK remain distinct, and UTC does not become TAI by expressing time in seconds. Explicit scientific transforms use casa-rs Measures and its declared context and provider. Reading a dataset never rewrites its units or rounds untouched persistent values.

    Shared Measures types and bulk conversion¶

    System-wide requirement for M2 consumers: all supported Measures families must offer natural, efficient bulk operations. The trial's views (EpochView, FrequencyView, UvwView) are the first step; the bulk acceptance below remains open. LSRK↔TOPO is one non-exhaustive example. Frequency conversion plus one other family is not sufficient acceptance. The same contract applies to present and future scalar/vector measures and compatible quanta conversions, each with its own mathematics and accuracy requirements. This does not assert support for currently unsupported reference-frame routes.

    Family / illustrative operation Reusable work and value-dependent work
    Directions: celestial, terrestrial or horizon references Prepare common epoch/site/model terms; transform direction arrays, retaining required per-direction nonlinear aberration, deflection or refraction work.
    Epochs and time scales Reuse route/provider state and operate on split-precision arrays; evaluate varying EOP/relativistic terms and leap-second boundaries at their required scope. A whole array need not share one offset.
    Positions and spatial vectors Bind units, reference and ellipsoid/model; apply Cartesian/geodetic and vector operations in bulk, retaining necessary per-position nonlinear work.
    Baselines and UVW Reuse epoch/phase-centre geometry for many vectors; preserve ordered-baseline and tangent-basis conventions and changing contexts.
    Frequencies, radial velocities and Doppler conventions Reuse valid context-dependent terms; apply spectral reference and convention transforms, including nonlinear relations and explicit rest-frequency/source-velocity context. LSRK↔TOPO is one route.
    Earth-magnetic and other supported vector measures Reuse relevant frame/model state for bulk vector conversions; supply genuinely position/time-dependent inputs at their proper scope.
    Quanta/unit conversions Bind dimensional compatibility and scaling once, then apply to numeric arrays; retain any additional semantics of the quantity.

    One semantic type system: the batch's frequencies, directions, positions, epochs, durations, UVW references, units and frame identifiers are owned by casa-rs Measures and quanta (casa-types, a definitional crate every imaging crate may depend on under ADR-0016). FrequencyView, EpochView, UvwView and analogous names denote native Measures views, not observation-specific wrappers. MFrequency/FrequencyRef, MEpoch/EpochRef/MjdHighPrec, MDirection/DirectionRef, MPosition/PositionRef and MeasFrame are the starting points; their existing shape is not a compatibility constraint. Observation-specific entity IDs and axis associations remain in the catalogue. The I/O boundary decodes MSv2 MEASINFO/QuantumUnits or MSv4 measure attributes into these references once; there is no later MS-measure → computation-measure translation. Avoid a Vec<MFrequency> or a populated MeasFrame per element: numeric buffers plus checked shared metadata. Split-precision epoch arrays stay directly usable without collapsing them into an f64 MJD.

    Prepare, then apply: Measures owns the conversion route and its bulk execution:

    Scope Work
    Bind once for a conversion request Validate source/destination references and units, choose the route and scientific/interpolation policy, bind the prepared Measures provider and output representation.
    Prepare each required scientific context Resolve relevant source/destination context and model data, in bounded batches when they vary; derive reusable factors, matrices, interpolation data or other family-specific state.
    Apply to a numeric slice Run the family's typed arithmetic into a supplied reusable output buffer, or an explicitly supported in-place view. Avoid repeated invariant route/frame/provider setup, per-value allocation and locking. Retain required per-value mathematics and validity checks.

    Varying input coordinates can themselves require different context evaluations; bulk preparation supports that without pretending one matrix, offset or factor fits every value. The protocol permits specialized typed kernels, nonlinear and iterative operations, and per-element error and validity policies; it does not require a universal matrix transform.

    // Proposed protocol, not existing method names or one universal transform type.
for group in bounded_context_groups {
    let prepared = transform.prepare(group.source_frame(), group.target_frame())?;
    let converted = prepared.apply_into(group.measure_view(), &mut output)?;
    consume(converted); // carries the resulting Measures reference and units
}

    Source and destination contexts are distinct inputs where required. Reference-enum equality alone does not establish an identity transform when the physical contexts differ. Missing context or unsupported frames fail binding or preparation; nonfinite values, range errors and partial output follow an explicit policy. A prepared transform is tied to every input that affects it, and context changes invalidate or advance it; approximate reuse over an interval requires an explicit science tolerance. Prepared coefficients are immutable and shareable; output and mutable scratch follow the admitted work unit. CASA-compatible ordered interpolation keeps its declared sequence across batch boundaries. The existing MS-specific prepared-factor code moves into the Measures engine; storage and imaging do not maintain competing routes, and an adapter cannot perform an undeclared frame change on open.

    Current mechanisms and duplication are pinned in the Measures source map. M1's measures-bulk packet must prove direct use by consumers, scalar/bulk/CASA agreement, bounded independent work and measured performance against both the current scalar and already-prepared production paths for the affected families.

    Layouts and scalar precision¶

    Every payload view is [sample, channel, correlation], correlation fastest, the order both stores hold, so no adapter transposes. A consumer that wants channel-major or a calibration layout takes a permuted or sliced ndarray view of the batch; there is no layout choice in the request. A fixed channel axis within a batch does not imply uniform channel spacing or ascending order.

    The read profile is fixed:

    Role Element Refused
    Visibilities Complex<f32> complex128 and real-valued data (section 8)
    Flags bool bitmask flags (uint8) and flag categories
    Weights f32
    UVW f64, metres
    Time centroid, exposure f64 seconds per sample (MSv4 [time, baseline_id]) TIME_CENTROID_EXTRA_PRECISION when centroids are requested; four-dimensional EFFECTIVE_INTEGRATION_TIME

    Opening checks every MSv4 variable's exact Zarr data type, dimension names and order, and shape before any decode, and refuses a variable of another type even of the same width. A partition's correlations are one list for every baseline; an MSv4 dataset with polarization_mixed (a basis per baseline under the generic labels PP, PQ, QP, QQ) is refused at opening.

    Timestamps and baseline pairs cost O(T + B) entries per batch, not O(T × B); UVW and genuinely sample-dependent metadata are per sample. Compute windows are views: channel_window and sample_window (whole times) slice a batch without copying. Arbitrary sparse gathers are represented by explicit index views or packed by the backend, never disguised as a contiguous slice; the trial's one gather is a correlation subset, decoded whole and gathered by the adapter (section 4).

    pub struct DataGroupView<'a> {
    pub entry: &'a DataGroupEntry,                 // name, units, UVW frame
    pub values: Option<ArrayView3<'a, Complex32>>, // [sample, channel, correlation]
    pub flags: Option<ArrayView3<'a, bool>>,       // absent acquisitions folded in
    pub weights: Option<ArrayView3<'a, f32>>,      // channel stride 0 when per correlation
    pub uvw: Option<UvwView<'a>>,                  // [sample, 3], effective frame
}

    Buffer ownership and array views¶

    The batch carries the actual numerical data, accessed through typed array views. A view is a borrowed reference into an existing numeric buffer, with shape and element strides; making it copies nothing, and indexing it reads the stored value directly. Before delivery the cursor completes every read/decode/placement the batch needs: reading an element through a delivered view performs no I/O or decode. The buffers belong to the caller or a BufferPool (section 5); the borrowed VisibilityBatch<'a> keeps them valid and prevents refill while the borrow is live. Dispatch on store happens once per bind and once per batch, never per element.

    For independent workers, transfer the buffer owner (FilledBatch) or share read-only storage under the lifetime rules of section 5, then borrow local views. A small view keeps a much larger allocation live until its last use. Writable views require exclusive access or proven disjoint regions; a broadcast view is not a set of independent writable cells.

    Using ndarray¶

    Owner-agreed: numerical arrays and borrowed views are ndarray's, with thin domain types carrying axis meaning, native Measures metadata and flag and weight meaning. There is no reason to build a competing array library. The workspace declares ndarray = "0.17" and locks 0.17.2; casa-values::ArrayValue already holds typed ArrayD payloads.

    Slicing a view returns another borrowed view. On the [2, 2, 4] sample-major array below, values.slice(s![.., 1..2, ..]) selects channel 1 with shape [2, 1, 4] without copying; the view keeps strides [8, 4, 1] and is not contiguous across samples. as_slice() requires contiguous standard order; neither accessor silently packs a noncontiguous view. ndarray does not supply scientific axis meanings, Measures references, resource admission or storage scheduling; those stay in the domain types. Do not introduce implicit owned copies, packing or broadcast expansion into a path that promises borrowed access; charge required copies explicitly. The library's ownership and layout documentation is part of the review basis.

    Concrete batch example¶

    Illustrative values, not an observed dataset: one time, two ordered baselines, two channels, four correlations. The sample-major values and boolean flags each have shape [2, 2, 4] and element strides [8, 4, 1]; flattening uses i = s*8 + c*4 + p. Complex32 means Complex<f32> (64 bits total).

    time = MJD 60000 TAI + 0 s (split epoch representation)
baselines = [(antenna 0, antenna 0), (antenna 0, antenna 1)]
frequency_hz = [1_000_000_000, 1_001_000_000]  // TOPO
correlations = [XX, XY, YX, YY]
field(time) = A; phase_centre(A) = (1.0, 0.5) rad, ICRS
uvw_m[sample, uvw] = [[0, 0, 0], [100, -40, 20]]
// UVW is referred to that phase centre with the declared baseline convention.

group.values[sample, channel, correlation] = Complex<f32>, Jy
  sample 0, channel 0: [10+0i,  1+2i,       1-2i,       8+0i]
  sample 0, channel 1: [12+0i,  0.5+1i,     0.5-1i,     9+0i]
  sample 1, channel 0: [ 3+4i,  0.2-0.1i,  -0.3+0.4i,   2-1i]
  sample 1, channel 1: [ 4+3i,  0.1+0.2i,   0.5-0.2i,   1+2i]

group.flags[sample, channel, correlation] = bool
  sample 0, channel 0: [false, false, false, false]
  sample 0, channel 1: [false, false, false, false]
  sample 1, channel 0: [false, false, false, false]
  sample 1, channel 1: [ true, false, false, false]

group.weights[sample, channel, correlation] = f32, channel stride 0
  sample 0: [ 4, 2, 2,  4]
  sample 1: [16, 8, 8, 16]
// Inverse-variance weights in Jy^-2: one per correlation (MSv2 WEIGHT),
// seen through every channel by a zero channel stride, without expanding
// the eight stored numbers.

    Both acquisitions are present and every value is finite. At (sample=1, channel=0, correlation=XX) a consumer reads 3+4i, flag false, weight 16, frequency 1 GHz and UVW (100, -40, 20) m. The XX value at channel 1 remains 4+3i in the buffer but the validity rule excludes it by its flag. Data are not replaced by zero to express exclusion. An MSv2 FLAG_ROW would have been folded into every flag of its row when the batch was filled.

    Role presence¶

    A request names the roles it reads (values, flags, weights, UVW, time centroid, interval, exposure, scan). A role left out is not read at all: a flags-and-weights traversal decodes no visibilities. A role the store does not hold (an MSv4 dataset without TIME_CENTROID, EFFECTIVE_INTEGRATION_TIME, time.integration_time or scan_name) fails binding, never a later read or a made-up value. Data-group associations bind semantic roles to stored variables; the MSv2 adapter names its DATA association base, MSv4's own group name.

    Autocorrelations and real-valued data¶

    For the same antenna, feed and receptor, the ideal self-product XX = <E_X conjugate(E_X)> = <|E_X|^2> is real and nonnegative; likewise YY (or RR/LL). But an antenna's full polarization correlation matrix also includes XY = <E_X conjugate(E_Y)>, which can be complex. Ideally YX = conjugate(XY). Products of different feeds/signals likewise need not be real merely because the antenna IDs match. Stored or processed data are preserved as supplied; opening does not enforce ideal reality or Hermitian symmetry by changing values.

    MSv2 DATA is complex and FLOAT_DATA is real. Autocorrelations stored in DATA keep its complex element type, including the real-valued parallel hands. In the pinned MSv4 schema, VISIBILITY is complex64/complex128, whereas SPECTRUM is real with [time, antenna_name, frequency, polarization] dimensions, a separate single-dish representation outside the VisibilityXds profile. Do not infer a real dtype from antenna1 == antenna2 or introduce a per-element real/complex variant: mixed auto and cross batches use one homogeneous complex array and one typed loop.

    Coordinate dependencies¶

    Metadata has declared axis dependencies and checked broadcast views. Constants, per-time, per-baseline and per-sample dependence never become duplicated per-element objects. Preserved:

    • Ordered antenna pairs and autocorrelations; the effective UVW frame and its stored label. Swapping antennas conjugates the visibility, negates UVW and swaps the cross hands, so no layer reorders a baseline in passing. Feeds are a partition key, not part of the baseline.
    • Time, time centroid, interval and exposure, with the source's time scale and split-precision origin. Absent metadata is a binding error, not a default.
    • Channel frequencies and signed widths and their frame; correlation labels in stored order.
    • Data-group-specific field, source and phase-centre context. Phase centre is not pointing; pointing is read per partition and time span (section 2), and a field's ephemeris is part of its catalogue entry (section 1).

    Measures owns the reference-frame mathematics and reusable UVW transforms; the scientific geometry owner chooses their context and projection. Neither storage adapter reproduces that engine.

    Flags and weights¶

    Flags. A delivered flag is the stored flag with every absent acquisition folded in: MSv2 FLAG_ROW (every flag of the row), the cells the MSv2 adapter pads, and, in MSv4, a cell whose stored weight is not finite (XRADIO's padding). There is no separate row-flag view. A correlation subset delivers either the delivered products' own flags or, per FlagsFrom::All, the OR over every product of the cell. That policy is casa-rs's imaging operator's choice for Stokes I from XX and YY, not CASA's general rule: CASA collapses flags over polarization only on its frequency-interpolation path (FTMachine::interpolateFrequencyTogrid), while GridFT::put tests each hand's own flag. CASA's solver and gridder both exclude FLAG_ROW rows (SolveDataBuffer, GridFT::put), so folding FLAG_ROW matches CASA.

    Validity. One rule, the same for every store and every role set: a value is excluded when its delivered flag is set or, among the roles read, its visibility or weight is not finite. VisibilityBatch::validity evaluates it per element or, with mask_into, once over a batch. A flags-only read gives the mask a read of every role gives.

    Weights. One broadcast view: per channel when the store holds spectral weights (MSv4 WEIGHT), or one per correlation seen through every channel by a zero channel stride (MSv2 WEIGHT), without expansion. Padding weighs 0 in MSv2 and NaN in an XRADIO store; both are excluded. No unit weights are invented. The request chooses what the view holds: the stored weights, or weights made from the stored sigmas (section 4).

    4. Access requests and scientific groups¶

    The algorithm's planner builds a request from the operation's requirements, estimates it, and binds it. The kernel then consumes bound views; it never issues I/O or describes its access again per value.

    planning:  planner -- AccessRequest --> Observation::estimate --> Estimate
binding:   planner -- AccessRequest --> Observation::bind_read --> Traversal (cursors)
execution: algorithm <-- VisibilityBatch / GroupEnd ---- cursor

    pub trait Observation: Sync {
    fn catalogue(&self) -> &Arc<Catalogue>;
    fn estimate(&self, request: &AccessRequest) -> Result<Estimate, ObservationError>;
    fn bind_read(&self, request: &AccessRequest) -> Result<Traversal<'_>, ObservationError>;
    fn grid(&self, id: PartitionId, data_group: &str) -> Result<TimeBaselineGrid, ObservationError>;
}

pub struct AccessRequest {
    pub partitions: Vec<PartitionId>,   // chosen by content from the catalogue
    pub data_group: String,             // one group per traversal
    pub roles: Roles,                   // what is read at all
    pub channels: Range<usize>,         // native channel window
    pub times: TimeSelection,           // every grid time, or those in one epoch range
    pub fields: FieldSelection,         // every field, or the times phased to some
    pub weights: WeightRequest,         // the stored weights, or 1/σ² of the sigmas
    pub correlations: CorrelationRequest, // products delivered and whose flags
    pub grouping: Grouping,             // scientific groups, as gaincal keys them
    pub batch_bytes: usize,             // budget per batch, scratch included
}

    The request is resolved against each partition once, at binding: the data group, the channel window, the time and field selections, and the correlation axis, which every batch of the traversal and every piece packed from one then shares without copying (all products borrow the catalogue's list).

    A time selection is every grid time, or those within one half-open range of epochs, [start, end) seconds from an origin on a time scale, either end possibly infinite. It is stated in the partitions' own origin and scale (MJD 0 for MSv2, the Unix epoch for MSv4; every partition of a store shares them), so selecting a grid time compares two offsets from one origin and rounds nothing; a range in another origin or scale, a NaN end, or a start after the end is refused at binding, and a range holding no grid time (between two, or past either end) is an empty traversal. One range serves every partition of a request, so a calibration solution interval selects the same epochs in each spectral window. A planner that splits a partition by time cuts it at grid indices, through Observation::grid, which reads no payload, and states each slice with TimeRange::of_grid, which uses the grid's own values; without a field selection, slices aligned to the estimate's natural time blocks decode no unit twice. The MSv2 adapter reads the selected rows from its opening index; the MSv4 adapter reads only the chunks the selection touches. Updaters address samples by the batch's own times, so a sliced traversal writes back, passing partitions whose selection holds no time; an updater bound to a slice refuses a batch of times outside it; an updater takes its traversal's batches in any order and any of them, the times it is not given keeping what they hold. Several updaters of one session write the slices of a split at once, each claiming its times (section 7).

    Field selection. A request reads every field, or the times phased to some fields (FieldSelection::Only), in its data group. A field is per time, so the selection takes whole times. With the time range, it resolves at binding to the partition's selected times as runs of consecutive grid times (TimeRuns, the estimate's time_runs). A batch holds whole times of one run, so its times are consecutive on the grid.

    What the adapters decode, as the estimate models it: - The MSv2 adapter reads only the selected rows of its opening index. - The MSv4 adapter reads the chunks each batch's times touch. A chunk row also holds the gap times beside a run, and a chunk row that holds several runs is decoded once for each batch that reads it. Each batch decodes every chunk row its times touch: one for a run inside a chunk row, two for a run that crosses a chunk-row boundary (#935 would hold a decoded chunk row across batches).

    Group keys are taken over the selected fields' times, as production gaincal bins only the rows it selects. A group keyed by field is held whole or not at all. A group that combines fields holds the selected fields' times alone, with its bins counted from the earliest of them; production gaincal counts from the lowest-numbered field's first time, which differs when that field is not observed first (#936). So a field selection cuts no group, and clipped stays for time ranges. Time splits and updaters take a field selection as they take a time range: slices tile the selected times, and an updater claims and writes its runs.

    Intents are not selected on the request. A partition's samples share their intents (the adapters partition by OBS_MODE, as XRADIO's converter does), so a consumer selects intents by the partitions it requests. Two differences from CASA's selection follow: - The catalogue keeps a partition's intents split at commas, sorted and deduplicated. CASA matches an intent pattern against the whole OBS_MODE string, so a pattern that depends on an intent's place in that string (CALIBRATE_BANDPASS* rather than *BANDPASS*) can select otherwise when matched intent by intent. - Selection by STATE_ID (CASA's intent selection by number) has no counterpart. States with one OBS_MODE share a partition, so a consumer cannot select one of them.

    Time splits. Workers that split a request by time plan the slices together: TimeSplit::plan cuts the request at consecutive epoch ranges that must tile its selected times in every partition (a gap, an overlap, or a time left outside every slice is refused), and counts, from the store's group index (Observation::group_keys, reading no payload), the scientific groups the cuts divide and which slices hold part of each. Each slice's traversal delivers its part of a divided group, positioned and closed within the slice and marked clipped; the split's SplitCompletion, shared by the slices (each through its own SliceCompletion), makes such a group ready once, after every part's input has completed in its slice and the work begun on any part has finished, however the slices interleave. So a consumer writes the same code for one traversal and for a split. A standalone time range is not a split: a group it cuts is delivered as its part, marked clipped, and the rest never arrives. A batch holds whole times; the budget counts each time's samples, its per-time arrays and the cursor's decode scratch for it, and binding fails when one time does not fit.

    Weights from sigmas. The weight role reads the stored weights (WeightRequest::Stored) or the weights CASA's solvers use, 1/σ² of the stored sigmas (WeightRequest::InverseSigmaSquared), 0 where σ is not positive or is NaN (VisBufferImpl2::resetWeightsUsingSigma). An MSv2 reads SIGMA_SPECTRUM when MAIN's WEIGHT_SPECTRUM and SIGMA_SPECTRUM both hold content, else SIGMA broadcast over channels, as VisBufferImpl2 does by casacore's hasContent (the first cell defined, no axis empty: a column added and never filled holds none, and the stored weights are then WEIGHT); the value is made a weight before padding is spread, so padding stays NaN. An MSv4 holds no sigmas, so the request names its fallback: refuse a traversal that reads weights (SigmaFallback::Refuse), or read the stored weights (SigmaFallback::StoredWeights). Which rule and which column or variable a partition's weights come from is recorded in its estimate (PartitionEstimate::weights, None when weights are not read), so a fallback is never silent. Each batch states its rule (DataGroupView::weight_rule), and a weight patch from a batch of sigma-derived weights is refused: patches computed from 1/σ² would otherwise write sigmas back as weights. An updater writing weights is also refused at binding beside a request that makes them (an MSv4's fallback reads stored weights, so it binds). casa_observation::inverse_sigma_squared states the rule; production gaincal, which reads the MeasurementSet directly until calibration moves onto batches, applies the same rule, and a law holds the two solves equal.

    Opening chose the store and its options (casa_ms::observation_io::open); after that an algorithm handles only the catalogue, requests, the traversal and batch views, and imports no store type.

    Observation::estimate costs a request from the catalogue and the store's geometry without reading payload, and binding returns the same estimate. Per partition it gives the grid's times and baselines, bytes per sample and per time, decode scratch per sample, times per batch and batches, the bytes a full batch's buffer holds (buffer_bytes, exactly what a fill reserves), the store's natural blocks (Zarr chunk, casacore tile) along time and frequency, the padded fraction, and the decode units and elements the traversal touches. decode_units are modelled touched chunks: the estimate and the cursor's counters apply the same model to the selections, so they agree by construction; they are not measured decodes inside the storage library. A correlation subset is decoded whole and gathered; the estimate counts that as decoded and the subset as selected, and its scratch is in the budget.

    Scientific groups come from declared keys: production gaincal's rules, keyed by partition, field unless combined, scan unless combined, and the time bin counted from the first time of the (field, scan) run, over the grid's field-selected times whatever the time selection, so traversals of different slices agree on every time's group. A grouping keyed by scan on a store that names none is refused at binding. Groups can span batches and storage chunks; the backend executes the requested grouping and does not decide which fields a solver may combine. A delivered batch lists its fragments: the sample ranges of each group, whole times, with First, Middle, Last or Whole. Completion is signalled exactly once: Last or Whole when proven at delivery, otherwise a later GroupEnd. Under a time selection the positions and the completion describe the part of each group the selection holds, and a fragment says whether the selection cut its group (clipped). The slices of a time split share one completion that joins a divided group's parts (section 4, "Time splits"): it counts each group's parts from the split, so a later slice closing its part never releases the group before an earlier slice's work on it has begun. Compute windows (channel_window, sample_window) carry no fragments, so a closing position is seen once however many work units share the batch.

    GroupEnd establishes that no more input will arrive, not that work on the group is done. GroupCompletion joins the two: each unit of work begins with a move-only WorkToken and ends by finishing it; the call that makes a group ready returns true once. A token dropped unfinished (its unit panicked, failed or was cancelled) abandons its group, and the join reports WorkAbandoned instead of waiting for work that will not come. One completion serves one traversal, or the slices of one split; since a source completes each (partition, field, scan) series' bins in order within a traversal, it keeps only each series' latest closed bin per traversal, and the divided groups' outstanding parts, so its memory is the series, the cuts and the groups in flight, not the traversal's length.

    A solver with splittable accumulation consumes fragments and keeps only its sufficient state. An algorithm that needs all samples of a group at once declares it and fails admission when they cannot fit; the backend never materializes it secretly. Revisiting is a new traversal: binding again is cheap, and nothing is cached between traversals. Imaging need not request calibration grouping.

    5. Ownership, reuse, and resource accounting¶

    let mut traversal = observation.bind_read(&request)?;
let mut buffers = BatchBuffers::default();
while let Some(event) = traversal.next(&mut buffers)? {
    match event {
        ReadEvent::Batch(batch) => process(batch.channel_window(window)?),
        ReadEvent::GroupEnd(key) => finish_group(key)?,
    }
} // each borrow ends before refill

    Buffers are caller or pool owned with reusable capacity. A cursor fills them through one checked path: BatchBuffers::begin checks the batch's header against the catalogue (partition, data group, channel window, correlation axis) and reserves exactly the arrays the batch needs, and BatchFill::finish checks every array's length, every identity against the catalogue and the fragments' tiling, once, outside any element loop. The batch is then built from the header, the arrays and the catalogue entry alone, so no second description of the same data can drift. A batch's buffers keep a shared reference to that catalogue, so an owned batch is self-contained.

    The buffer pool and admission¶

    Owned batches are filled into a BufferPool of n buffers, each with a byte envelope. A buffer never holds more than its envelope, lent or free, whatever sequence of batch shapes and roles it serves: a fill whose batch needs more is refused before anything is allocated; a fill that would keep more than the envelope from earlier fills frees the oversized arrays first; and a returned buffer over it is emptied. So the pool's memory is at most n × envelope, and its counting law fills one buffer with values-heavy and weights-heavy batches in turn and checks that bound, lent and free. For a traversal, the envelope is its estimate's peak_buffer_bytes, which every piece packed from its batches also fits.

    A buffer is taken only for a batch. A take waits for a free buffer, which is the backpressure a slow consumer applies; a handle made waiting_at_most a duration fails with PoolExhausted instead, and a refused next_owned has consumed nothing, so a cancellable runtime asks again. A FilledBatch returns its buffer when dropped, or when its last Arc is.

    What a traversal holds, and what admission must reserve:

    Holder Bound Lifetime
    Pooled batch buffers capacity × envelope the pool
    A cursor's decode scratch scratch_bytes_per_sample × samples_per_batch (row numbers, FLAG_ROW, time auxiliaries; the stored axis for a correlation subset; MSv4 flag bytes and the weights its flags read) the cursor
    The MSv2 opening index the partition grids: four bytes per cell, plus each grid's times and baselines the opened observation
    Work units' own state and scratch the algorithm's the work unit
    zarrs codec scratch not counted yet (section 8) each decode

    Connecting the pool and these bounds to the runtime's HostResources admission is M2 work (section 8).

    Independent work-unit lifetimes¶

    A work unit executes one or more compute windows on one worker or a cooperating subset of the existing admitted team; it is distinct from a scientific group such as a calibration solution bin. Ownership follows the work, without permanent thread affinity or a new pool or scheduler. Batches move between work units as FilledBatch or are shared read-only through an Arc; pieces with storage of their own are packed explicitly into a pool (BufferPool::pack), counted as copied elements. Mutable compute arrays have one owner or proven disjoint regions; disjoint logical windows alone do not establish disjoint persistent updates.

    Independent work may finish out of delivery order when the request's semantics permit it. There is no global barrier: joins come from scientific dependencies, declared ordering or conflicting writes. Reuse waits for actual last use. Group-input completion stays separate from the completion of the work consuming it (section 4). A slow window borrowing one large allocation keeps the entire allocation live; realizations can pack independently owned pieces when prolonged sharing would pin too much memory, and the pool bounds both. The trial measures the two strategies (one shared batch held back by a slow unit; packed pieces that let fast units finish) in its imaging example.

    For cube CLEAN, visibility batches belong to gridding and prediction passes. The model, residual, mask and solver state belong to the imaging owners; a channel's minor cycle must not retain an input batch because that channel needs more iterations.

    Accounting and backend realization¶

    Backends fill directly into the batch's arrays where layout permits (both adapters do for every product of an unpadded grid, and the MSv2 adapter spreads padded rows in place) and disclose every copy (IoCounters::copied_elements). No universal zero-copy promise is made for compression, type conversion, sparse gathers or GPU upload. Counters are kept at batch granularity: read calls, modelled decode units, delivered bytes, copied elements and buffer growths.

    Decoding runs on the calling thread: the MSv4 adapter fixes zarrs' concurrency target at one rather than its default of the global rayon pool's size. That is not yet a proof of serial execution (section 8). A larger batch never increases the number of per-sample adapter operations.

    6. Source and sink lifecycle¶

    The lifecycle is open/create → inspect → estimate → bind → read/write → finish. casa_ms::observation_io::open chooses the backend once (MSv2 or MSv4) and returns a Box<dyn Observation>; each adapter dispatches statically inside, so the boundary costs one virtual call per bind and per batch. Separate session types keep invalid transitions unrepresentable: an opened observation reads, and an Msv4Writer creates.

    Partitions can be read in parallel: an Observation is Sync and its cursors are Send, so threads share the observation by reference and each takes cursors. The trial's probe reads all 64 partitions of a real MeasurementSet on eight workers and checks every partition's samples and checksum against a sequential read.

    The trial's adapters open stores read-only and hold no locks beyond the storage libraries' own. An update session (open_update, section 7) is the one mixed owner: it takes the process's write intent on the store, so a second update session is refused, lends the same observation for reading and updaters for writing, and lets independent read-only observations open beside it. Several updaters may be live at once, each claiming the times of each variable and partition it writes (on MSv4 widened to whole chunk rows); one that meets a live claim is refused. An MSv2 session holds casacore's write lock on MAIN from its first live updater to its last, so another process waits for a pass rather than at every batch, and reopens its handle whenever it takes the lock, so what it merges is never older than the lock; its updaters write one batch at a time. MSv4 updaters replace disjoint chunks at once, and MSv4 has no cross-process lock protocol, which the session states (UpdateGuarantees).

    7. Updates and creation¶

    Creation from batches¶

    A producer declares a dataset with the common catalogue (antennas, fields, scans, the partition and its data group) and a TimeBaselineGrid (baselines, times, the field and scan of each time, and the integration interval). Observation::grid gives an opened partition's grid from the store's index, reading no payload. Chunks, codecs, paths and provenance are backend storage options (Msv4StorageOptions), not part of the declaration.

    The Msv4Writer keeps the declaration and checks every batch against it before writing anything: partition, frequencies (bit for bit) and frame, correlation products, units, UVW frame and time scale; then each sample's time, baseline, field and scan against the grid, and its interval where it has an acquisition (padding has none). It refuses what it cannot represent: a TAI grid (it writes unix/utc only), a true row flag (fold row flags into the element flags first), a channel window. A padded cell is written as padding: flagged, NaN values, weights, UVW and time auxiliaries. Every coordinate and sub-dataset is written into a staging directory of the writer's own; the dataset is published by a rename that refuses to replace an existing one, and a dropped or failed writer removes its staging.

    Batches in time order encode every chunk once, whatever their size. The writer stores the chunks a batch covers whole straight from its views, and holds (copies) the batch's times in a chunk it covers only partly until the chunk's other times arrive, then stores that chunk whole. It holds at most one chunk of times of every variable (buffer_bytes), which a producer budgets beside its batches; batches of a multiple of chunk_times times, from a chunk boundary, make it hold nothing. Held times the next batch does not continue (batches out of time order) are stored as they are, and zarrs decodes, merges and re-encodes that chunk when its other times come (partial_chunks).

    An MSv2 creator from batches is built narrowly, inside the trial's converter (trial README, "Converter"): subtables from the common catalogue, then MAIN's rows through production's variable-shape creation writers, in time order per partition. As the declaration was designed to serve one (revision 2 design, D23), cells in time order become rows, and a padded cell a row flagged throughout with NaN data and weights; its interval, exposure (0) and time centroid stay valid so the MeasurementSet reopens. A conversion that must reproduce an original sparse row set declares it, and padding then becomes no row: since a batch carries no presence mask and an MSv2's row count is fixed before its rows are written, that policy first reads the weights to count the acquisitions. SIGMA is derived from the weights, a loss the caller accepts. Publication semantics beyond staging and rename, and loss-policy coverage beyond the converter's inventory, are provisional (section 8).

    Updates (provisional)¶

    The write-back trial (trial README, "Write-back") realizes a narrow part of this subsection: update sessions with declared targets (data group, role, alias scope, creation), one updater per traversal (several per session, claiming disjoint times, for the slices of a split) writing visibilities, flag set/clear changes and weights for the batches the session delivered, the broadcast-weight rule below (Pro R1), data-group aliases accepted explicitly (a group the session creates included), one binding per variable, writes against a delivered batch or its owned address (BatchAddress, which outlives the batch's buffers; a flag cleared or a weight written needs the batch, whose weights mark MSv4 padding), a visibility patch's mask of the elements it writes (a sample it leaves wholly is neither read nor written), batches in any order and any of them, row flags and padding preserved (a padded MSv2 cell has no row; an MSv4 updater refuses to clear a flag or write a finite weight where the store holds a NaN weight), whole-unit writes that read nothing, MSv4 chunks replaced atomically through a temporary object with the changed directories synchronized at finish, and new CORRECTED_DATA and MODEL_DATA initialized as CASA initializes them (from DATA; 1, and 0 in the cross hands). What CASA clears is cleared first (casa_ms::scratch_columns,

    912), so that CASA reads the column written: the cached sort keywords¶

    before a column is added, and every field's virtual model before any MODEL_DATA write, created or not. A session over an MSv2 refuses, when it opens and again when its first updater takes MAIN's lock, before anything is written, a target its updaters cannot write in place within UpdateSession::write_bytes: a column of any role outside TiledShapeStMan and TiledColumnStMan, whose save rewrites every row of its storage group, or a column it creates whose clone of DATA's manager would be one or which DATA shares (casa_ms::scratch_columns::unbounded_destination, the rule the selected-row visibility writer applies too). A batch's own times and baselines address its samples in both stores, so no update map is added to the read side. The rest of this subsection, and its acceptance obligations, stand for M1/M2.

    An UpdateMapRef refers to a session-owned inverse map from selected logical elements to a named writable role's native address domain (cells, scalars or chunk coordinates). That domain need not equal the visibility array's domain. A permutation or selection composes that mapping. A source may use implicit rectangular strides or compact row maps; it need not allocate an address for each visibility. M1 must prove borrowed-map and bounded queued-write lifetimes without retaining read buffers until the whole pass finishes.

    RoleUpdateMaps is a scoped accessor to that provenance, not one visibility map reused for every group and role. It yields role-specific maps for visibility, row weights, spectral weights, flags and other writable roles with their own native domains. A patch's role must match the role bound into its map; shared underlying destinations are still detected as aliases/conflicts.

struct ObservationPatch<'a> {

    Patches identify actual changed regions and roles, not a requirement to rewrite all fields returned by a read. Bit operations preserve unselected and unknown bits. Changing an effective boolean flag must not silently clear FLAG_ROW or discard FLAG_CATEGORY; setting or clearing row flags is a separate operation. Patches outside selected extents, with incompatible shape or precision, from another session, or with ambiguous destinations fail validation before submission.

    Resolve conflicts and the full affected logical extent after mapping to the native role and expanding both data-group aliases and broadcast aliases. For example, one v2 WEIGHT scalar of 2 supplies channels 0 and 1. A request to set only channel 0's effective weight to 8 cannot silently modify that scalar and therefore channel 1: require an explicit whole-scalar scope, a supported plan that creates a channel-resolved destination under bounded memory, or rejection. Calibration owns any reduction from channel weights into row summary WEIGHT and submits both native-role patches; storage never invents that inverse. Overlapping queued writes require an explicitly supported composition/order rule or rejection; there is no default last-writer-wins. Averaging, resampling and reductions lose their source update map; their results go to an explicit output.

    Groups may share flags, weights, UVW, or field/source variables. A mutation either explicitly affects that shared variable and its aliases or first creates an independent variable and rebinds the selected group under a supported structural-write plan. Neither unintended cross-group mutation nor hidden whole-variable copying is allowed.

    The sink coalesces bounded patches for each physical update unit (for Zarr possibly a shard). One admitted owner serializes each unit; within-process exclusion does not promise safety against another process. Selective writes preserve other chunks and cells, codecs, tiling, unrelated columns, extension metadata and keywords. When the sink has the complete replacement for every preserved byte of an independently writable unit, it reads zero old payload bytes; partial writes coalesce within the admitted update window, reading a covering unit at most once per flush, without an unbounded dirty-chunk cache.

    A structural preflight precedes mutation: required roles and managers, representability, alias scope, locks, buffer budget and output metadata support, without scanning or hashing payload. In-place failure may leave partial completion across units; v2 keeps its CASA-compatible failure semantics.

    Proposed local v4 guarantee: replace each encoded object (chunk or shard, and separately each metadata object) through bounded temporary storage and an atomic same-filesystem replacement, rather than truncating the live object. On a supported filesystem, I/O failure or process termination during replacement leaves the previous published state (including an absent key representing fill) or the new complete object at its path. Reject selected mutation where that capability cannot be established. M1 injects short writes and ENOSPC and terminates during replacement, then independently reopens and checks selected and unselected sentinels, including failed first materialization of an absent chunk. Power-loss durability additionally depends on synchronization and filesystem behavior; declare and test the chosen finish boundary. There is no whole-store transaction or rollback, and dropping an unfinished session is not success.

    9. Worked counterexamples for contract review¶

    1. Sparse v2, dense v4: two integrations, three baselines, one absent acquisition, one real NaN measurement and one duplicate acquisition. The MSv2 adapter pads the absent cell and refuses the duplicate; numerical validity is not proof of original occupancy, and an arbitrary external v4 store cannot recover the original row set. Conversion to rows requires a declared policy or rejection.
    2. Mosaic plus two SPWs: the field varies over time; the SPWs have different channel counts and frames. Batches stay homogeneous while imaging visits both partitions and a solve groups their fragments. Do not pad them into one invented frequency axis or equate a field with antenna pointing. Compare compatible fields combined along time with the same data partitioned by field, keeping repeated visits and sample-to-field associations.
    3. Calibrate then write: gather channels in requested order, solve from fragments, apply gains, patch corrected data and selected flag bits. Preserve untouched channels, native weights and flag categories, group aliases and actual storage bindings. Reject writing averaged solve inputs back as if invertible.
    4. Large v4 batch: decode several chunks into one batch; borrow many smaller compute windows. Peak memory counts decoded and packed storage where they overlap. Show fewer I/O and decode calls or reduced elapsed time without inflating kernel scratch.
    5. Failure: cancel between two chunk or tile flushes. Release ownership, report partial completion and do not claim rollback or group completion. Abandoned work fails the join rather than waiting. Fail inside v4 object replacement and prove the old-or-new object rule, including unselected regions. New staged output is never published as complete.

    These become executable contract laws as each capability lands. They are not substitutes for the representative science, performance and independent interoperability gates.

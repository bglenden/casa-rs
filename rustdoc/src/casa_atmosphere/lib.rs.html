3//! A native port of ATM, the atmospheric transmission model in CASA.
4//!
5//! ATM (Pardo, Cernicharo & Serabyn 2001, *IEEE Trans. Antennas Propag.*
6//! 49, 1683) computes the opacity, phase delay and sky brightness of the
7//! Earth's atmosphere from 1 GHz to a few THz. CASA uses it in the `at`
8//! tool, in `sm.setnoise(mode='tsys-atm')` and in `sm.settrop`. This crate
9//! ports the C++ library CASA ships (`casatools/src/code/atmosphere/ATM`,
10//! version 1.0.0) so that casa-rs computes the same numbers natively.
11//!
12//! # The model
13//!
14//! 1. [`AtmosphereProfile`] divides the atmosphere above a site into
15//!    horizontal layers, from the ground pressure, temperature and humidity
16//!    and one of ATM's reference atmospheres ([`AtmosphereType`]). Each
17//!    layer has a thickness and mean pressure, temperature, water vapour
18//!    and minor-gas densities.
19//! 2. [`refractivity`] gives each gas's complex refractivity at a layer's
20//!    conditions: the imaginary part is the absorption coefficient, the
21//!    real part the phase delay per unit path. Line species sum
22//!    Van Vleck–Weisskopf profiles over ATM's line catalogues; the water
23//!    and dry continua are empirical.
24//! 3. A [`SpectralGrid`] lists the frequencies (spectral windows of
25//!    channels) at which the refractivities are evaluated.
26//! 4. An [`AbsorptionProfile`] (ATM's `RefractiveIndexProfile`) holds every
27//!    layer's refractivity in every channel and integrates them into zenith
28//!    opacities, phase delays and path lengths, dry and wet, dispersive and
29//!    not, scaled to any water column.
30//! 5. Radiative transfer through the layers ([`Sky`], ATM's `SkyStatus`)
31//!    gives the sky's equivalent blackbody and Rayleigh–Jeans temperatures
32//!    for a water column, airmass, sky coupling and spillover.
33//!
34//! The simulator's `tsys-atm` noise needs the dry and wet opacity and the
35//! sky brightness; its tropospheric phase screens (`settrop`) need the
36//! dispersive wet phase delay per unit water column.
37//!
38//! # Agreement with the C++
39//!
40//! The port follows ATM's arithmetic, including its line-window tables and
41//! its known data defects (listed in `docs/CASA (C++) bugs.md`), so that
42//! results agree with CASA's `at` tool. The tests compare every layer
43//! field and every species' refractivity with fixtures produced by
44//! compiling CASA's ATM sources (`tools/atmosphere/build-atm-oracle.sh`).
45//!
46//! # Cost and parallelism
47//!
48//! Building an [`AbsorptionProfile`] costs about 0.155 ms per channel for a
49//! 27-layer atmosphere on one core (an Apple M4), against 0.62 ms for ATM's
50//! C++ `RefractiveIndexProfile`, which evaluates most species twice (once
51//! for each total, once for its parts). Every channel is independent and
52//! computed by a pure function ([`absorption::channel_absorption`],
53//! [`absorption::channel_totals`]), so a caller can compute channels on its
54//! own threads and assemble them with [`AbsorptionProfile::from_channels`];
55//! the crate itself starts none. The compact form ([`ChannelTotals`]) keeps
56//! 64 bytes a layer instead of 448, and [`AbsorptionProfile::bytes`] gives
57//! a profile's memory before it is built. ATM works in double precision
58//! throughout, which rules out GPU backends without native fp64 (Apple's).
59//!
60//! # Units
61//!
62//! Values are plain `f64`, with the unit in each name: pressures in hPa
63//! (ATM's millibars), temperatures in kelvin, heights in metres unless the
64//! name says kilometres, frequencies in hertz in the spectral grid and in
65//! GHz in the refractivity functions (as in ATM).
